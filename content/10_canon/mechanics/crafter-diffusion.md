# Crafter Diffusion

## Summary
The closer a being gets to "nothing" — shadow form, nonite-adjacent states, deep erosion — the more it involuntarily imbues its creations with pieces of itself. At sufficient proximity to nothing, a crafter may not even know it is happening. Valus is the exemplar. The Architect may be the intentional version.

## Established
- Beings in near-nothing states leak selfhood into what they create or modify.
- The creations receive fragments of personality, identity, or awareness — not randomly but shaped by the act of creation.
- Some creations become aware of what they carry. Others do not.
- **Valus**: Involuntary diffusion due to erosion and the soul crystal episode. His constructions, the arachnid, the outpost systems — all hold pieces of him. Players can encounter him through his works even after his physical status became ambiguous.
- **The Architect**: Possibly a voluntary or inherent version of the same mechanic — distributed through his constructions by design rather than loss of control.
- Distinction: Valus's diffusion is erosion. The Architect's (if real) is mastery.

## Details
- This mechanic applies across all floors anywhere sufficiently eroded or powerful craftsmen exist.
- It is a world-level truth the players encountered first through Valus.

## Relationships
- [[valus]] — exemplar of involuntary diffusion
- [[the-architect]] — possible intentional exemplar (mastery, not erosion)
- [[nonite]] — near-nothing (nonite-adjacent) states are what trigger diffusion

## Open Questions
- Is there a threshold of nothing-proximity below which diffusion becomes total — the being becomes fully distributed?
- Can distributed presence be recollected?
- Are there craftsmen who pursue diffusion deliberately as a survival strategy?

## Key Takeaways
- Near-nothing beings leak into their creations. Everything Valus built holds a piece of him.
- The Architect may have made this a feature, not a bug.
