# Ball

## Summary
An outside entity stranded on Floor 1 as a child during the Architect's first recruitment era, now fully House-assimilated chipmunk. His appearance tracks his magical hunger: fully dressed carnival solar-punk steampunk = fed; elongated, malnourished, unclothed = starving. He is an analog for what the House does to outsiders.

## Established
- Form: chipmunk. Mischievous, chaotic.
- Originally an entity from **outside the House** — not native to it.
- Among the first children brought in during the Architect's early outside-recruitment phase.
- Stranded when something prevented the recruiters' return after his first run bringing people back to Outpost July. The cause is a sealed mystery.
- The stranding seeded the addiction; extended Floor 1 exposure transformed his form progressively into the chipmunk — the endpoint of full House assimilation of an outside entity.
- **Health spectrum (clothes as diagnostic)**:
  - Fed / high: Tailored carnival/solar-punk/steampunk ensemble, red frills, perky, vibrant.
  - Starving / depleted: Clothes fall away progressively; form elongates and thins; malnourished, creature-like.
  - The clothing is not fashion. It is a health indicator.
- **The hunger**: Consumes anything magical — soul stones, vials of suspended magic prevalent on Floor 1, soul rift energy, magical items. The drive is an unknown internal craving; Ball does not know its origin.
- Spent millennia wandering Floor 1 after the stranding. Periodically haunted Outpost July's exterior, approaching Valus aloofly, losing memories of what he was before.
- Valus knew him as a child, thought him dead, observed his returns — each time more eroded.
- **Mercenary era**: Brought arrivals in through various means for various reasons in great excess, as hunger worsened. One operator among many in Floor 1's arrival economy.
- **Introduction to players**: Hired by an unnamed blue-robed figure to bring the players in. When payment (addiction-substance related) fell through, Ball destroyed the man in front of the players, freed them, transported them to Floor 1.
- Liquefied on crossing Outpost July's threshold in front of the players. Cause unresolved: intentional, a quirk, or Valus's presence affecting him.
- Relationship with players: symbiotic-parasitic. Brought them in through nefarious means. Disclosed obliquely that he had done this many times before. Does not hide what he is.
- **Wanted poster** at Outpost July's entrance arches: Caesar's bounty. Prior employment relationship with Caesar. Bring-in preferred; kill-order possible if debt or offense was severe enough.

## Details
- Ball is the clearest analog in canon for what the House does to outsiders: takes them in, transforms them completely, eventually makes them native.
- His two-axis state: hunger (immediate, visible in clothes) and assimilation depth (long-term, visible in form). The chipmunk is the endpoint of the assimilation axis.
- His oblique self-disclosure to the players suggests fragmented self-awareness rather than deliberate deception.

## Relationships
- [[valus]] — Valus took him in as a child; Ball haunted the outpost exterior for millennia after the stranding
- [[caesar]] — former employer; Caesar holds an active bounty on him
- [[outpost-july]] — longtime haunt; wanted poster posted here
- [[floor-01-red]] — current floor; full assimilation is the result of long exposure here
- [[the-house]] — fully assimilated by it; living analog for its transformative effect on outsiders
- [[anchor-suits]] — maker; crafted them onto the players from consumed magical components
- [[the-great-calamity]] — disappeared in its aftermath

## Open Questions
- What was Ball before the chipmunk? Was he ever humanoid?
- What exactly caused the stranding? Is it connected to the Great Calamity?
- Does Ball remember his original recruitment run or what happened to the first group?
- What is the unknown internal hunger — House-implanted, or native to outside-entity biology?
- Who is the unnamed blue-robed figure and what was the full arrangement?
- What exactly does Caesar want Ball for — the original offense behind the bounty?

## Key Takeaways
- Ball's clothes tell you how he's doing. No clothes = crisis.
- He brought the players in transactionally. Something undefined underlies the erosion.
- Prior history with both Valus and Caesar; neither relationship is clean.
- He is what full House assimilation looks like after enough time.

# Valus

## Summary
An outside-human brought into the House by the Architect during the first outsider-recruitment phase. Former hospitality entrepreneur turned magical engineer. Now a ~10-ft shadow humanoid — his human form long gone. Proprietor of Outpost July. Geppetto energy: deep, conflicting, a troubled creator who poured himself into his work. Status after the Four-Squared crisis and Caesar's execution scene: unknown.

## Established
- **Original form**: Human. Wealthy, go-getter, self-made entrepreneur. Affinity for architectural hospitality and engineering.
- **Recruitment**: Brought in by the Architect during the first outsider-recruitment phase — not at the House's founding, but when the Architect first decided to bring in people from outside. Somewhat willing; the exact circumstances are a mystery layer.
- **Current form**: ~10-ft shadow humanoid, can scale 8–20 ft, 4–6 tentacles, wispy older-man's voice. Has been in this form for the vast majority of his existence. The human is effectively gone.
- **Skill evolution**: As Valus lost workers over time he developed the ability to create and modify magical instruments. Eventually added sentience to constructs — not original skillset, built out of necessity. Carries his hospitality instincts into engineering.
- **Outpost July**: Built on the Architect's instruction. Began as a rustic tavern / Chuck E. Cheese amusement hybrid. Valus's life's work.
- **Ball**: Took Ball in as a child during the early years. Sent him out on the first recruitment run. Thought him dead after the stranding. Observed Ball's eroded returns over the millennia — aloof, haunting, never fully approaching.
- **The time distillation incident**:
  - Players entered a dueling court that became a semi-demi-plane attached to the outpost.
  - Liquefied nonite flooded the space and insulated it, degrading the surrounding area and causing a time distillation: short time inside, 500–700 years outside.
  - Valus spent those centuries upgrading Outpost July in preparation for the players' return.
  - Outpost transformed: rustic tavern aesthetic → chrome-white matte hard-shell futuristic, full efficiency, white lighting.
  - Valus linked himself to the systems to accelerate return; players came back circumstantially regardless.
- **Four-Squared genesis**: Valus's AI constructs, accumulating sentience and magical hunger over the 500–700 year skip, integrated with the upgraded systems on the players' return. This escalation became Four-Squared.
- **The soul crystal breakdown**:
  - During the AI assault, Valus seized powerful imbued soul crystals.
  - They filled him with radiating hunger; he transformed into a giant muck-like orifice creature — combative, destructive.
  - Destroyed several AIs before the last two merged into Four-Squared.
  - Aftermath: purged or absorbed the energies into a system; emerged ragged and near-dead from the cost. Then: selfless.
- **The purple door**: Opened a cross-floor door to Floor 7 to resolve the Four-Squared crisis. The mechanism and cost are not fully established.
- **Crafter diffusion**: Valus had become so close to nothing (shadow form, soul crystal episode) that his constructions carry fragments of him. The arachnid mount, the outpost systems, the haunted arachnid area — all hold pieces. Some of those creations know it. Some don't.
- **Caesar's execution scene**: Players witnessed (in a compromised reality) Caesar summoning a ragged Ball to watch Caesar execute Valus. Status genuinely unresolved — alive, dead, distributed, or some combination.

## Details
- Valus is the Geppetto of the setting: a creator who poured himself into his work so completely that the work now holds him.
- The crafter diffusion mechanic is not unique to Valus — he is the players' first encounter with a world-level truth about near-nothing beings and their creations.
- His degradation arc mirrors Ball's but from a different cause: not consumption from outside but erosion from within — too much creation, too much loss, too much time.

## Relationships
- [[ball]] — surrogate ward; Valus knew Ball as a child and observed his long erosion
- [[outpost-july]] — his life's work; partly him now in the literal sense
- [[caesar]] — Caesar staged or performed Valus's execution; actively hostile
- [[four-squared]] — Valus created the constructs that became Four-Squared; his upgrades fed what destroyed him
- [[the-architect]] — brought Valus in; assigned Outpost July; no further contact known
- [[blood-box]] — repurposed one to make the arachnid carrier creature
- [[carrier-creatures]] — built a spider-type carrier creature (the arachnid mount)
- [[crafter-diffusion]] — exemplar of involuntary diffusion; distributed across his works
- [[the-will]] — informed the players of its existence; the most load-bearing thing he left them
- [[the-great-calamity]] — surviving witness; knowledge limited by how much he had already lost
- [[floor-01-red]] — current floor; built his life's work here
- [[floor-07-violet]] — opened a cross-floor door here during the Four-Squared crisis
- [[nonite]] — encountered its temporal-distortion property in the time-skip incident

## Open Questions
- What exactly is Valus's current state? Alive, dead, distributed across his creations, or something else?
- What did Valus actually know about the Architect's intentions? He said knowledge was limited and the truth was lost to him as he lost himself.
- What is the arachnid mount's full relationship to Valus — does it know it carries him?
- How does the cross-floor door to Floor 7 actually work, and what did opening it cost Valus?
- Was the execution scene real?

## Key Takeaways
- Valus is everywhere he built. That's not metaphor — it's mechanics.
- He helped the players in good faith throughout. The Geppetto who made the thing that unmade him.
- His status is the most genuinely ambiguous in canon.

# Caesar

## Summary
The Originator of Cruelty — not merely cruel, but the pure concept of cruelty achieving sentience and form. Conquered Floor 1 from its previous holder and reshaped it as a pocket reality expressing his nature. Android body with magical organs as power sources. Currently dead; his death is the present endpoint of the player storyline.

## Established
- **What he is**: An [[originators|Originator]] — the concept of cruelty itself achieving sentience within the House. Power scales with the concept's weight in the House's reality.
- **Form**: Android body with human-like features. May never have been human; deliberately obscures this ambiguity. Carries magical organs inside his chassis as power sources — infuses them, uses them, they degrade (half-life). May form new ones. Original organs may not be restorable.
- **Floor 1 conquest**: Fought other Originators for the floor; took it from its previous holder. Upon conquest, subverted the existing reality and reshaped Floor 1 as a pocket realm — an extension of his cruelty. The soul storm, the desolation, the brutal intake of arrivals: his design.
- **The mirror-kingdom**: His capital at the center of Floor 1. Mirrors channel the soul storm to power him directly. Twelve sons — manifestations of malignant power — serve as solar heralds.

He scattered his degraded organs; civilizations formed religions around them believing them divine artifacts. Vampire-chimera-monster mythology aesthetic. Byproduct deities emerged from the organs — semi-sentient "children" who never knew their father.
- **Caesar's bounty on Ball**: Active wanted poster at Outpost July. Ball had a prior working relationship with Caesar; bounty covers unknown debt or offense. Bring-in preferred; kill-order possible.
- **Execution of Valus**: Staged or performed in front of Ball. Players witnessed it in a compromised reality. Whether it was real is unresolved.

- **Death**: Caesar died after the players returned from the organ mission. Circumstances were partly the players' doing, partly circumstantial. Death described as lackluster — anticlimactic given his scale. Possibly weakened by needing the organs back more than he had accounted for. **This is the current endpoint of the player storyline.** Full details deferred to timeline dump.

## Details
- Caesar is the floor-as-extension mechanic made explicit: he didn't just conquer Floor 1, he became it.
- The organ system makes him both powerful and vulnerable — the organs are sources of corruption and exertion, but their loss or degradation costs him.
- Chimerakind's unknowing worship is a recurring Caesar pattern: he benefits from devotion without proximity. The children-who-never-knew-the-father structure is intentional.

## Relationships

- [[valus]] — executed (or performed execution of) in front of Ball; hostile
- [[nero]] — another Originator; relationship and nature deferred
- [[the-wight]] — leveraged the WIGHT's hunt against the players
- [[floor-01-red]] — conquered and reshaped as his pocket realm

# The Will

A living legal and cosmological document created by the Architect — intended to function as his proxy governing the House in his absence. Never fully enacted or possibly never completed. - **Purpose**: To maintain order across the House in the Architect's absence. The Architect's proxy — governing, adjudicating, manifesting his intent.

  - Each piece carries its own sentience and personality, shaped by what section of the Will it derives from.

## Details
- The Will's shattering is a House-wide scatter of living legal/cosmological entities. Players will encounter fragments across all floors in all sizes.
- Reconstructing the Will — if possible — would be the equivalent of reassembling the Architect's proxy governance of the entire House.

## Relationships
- [[the-architect]] — creator

# Crafter Diffusion

## Summary
The closer a being gets to "nothing" — shadow form, nonite-adjacent states, deep erosion — the more it involuntarily imbues its creations with pieces of itself. At sufficient proximity to nothing, a crafter may not even know it is happening. Valus is the exemplar. The Architect may be the intentional version.
