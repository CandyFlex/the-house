# Outpost July

## Summary
[[valus]]'s establishment on [[floor-01-red]]. One of twelve monthly outposts built across Floor 1 at the Architect's instruction — July is among the few survivors. Built into a 300-ft cliff face; non-Euclidean interior containing tavern, arcade, dueling courts, and lodging. Now functionally abandoned following Valus's apparent death and the Four-Squared crisis.

## Established
- **The name**: One of twelve outposts, each named for a calendar month. Most have been destroyed — Caesar eliminated some deliberately; attrition took others. July is a survivor. See [[outpost-system]].
- Located on a 300-ft wall facing the radial chasm of Floor 1.
- Exterior: steampunk arches; desolate-facing.
- Interior far larger than the exterior implies — extends deep into the mountain.
- Contains: tavern facade, banners, tables, drinks, vending (including a liminal vending machine styled as a small tavern); automaton staff.
- Six tiered amusement areas including dueling combat-sport courts with reward economies.
- Guest lodging.
- Built and maintained by [[valus]]; originally assigned by [[the-architect]].
- **The time-skip**: A nonite-insulation incident in the dueling courts caused a time distillation — short time inside, 500–700 years outside. Valus spent those centuries retrofitting the interior: original rustic tavern / Chuck E. Cheese aesthetic → chrome-white matte, hard-shell futuristic, full-efficiency design.
- Site of the Four-Squared crisis and Valus's soul crystal breakdown.
- **Current state**: Functionally abandoned after Valus's apparent death. Desolate. The futuristic retrofit remains but unmaintained.
- **Ball's presence**: Players returned to find Ball in a decrepit state — depleted, starving end of his health spectrum — near the outpost. He helped them leave.
- **Wanted poster**: Caesar's bounty on [[ball]] posted at the entrance arches.

## Relationships
- [[valus]] — built it, ran it, possibly still present in it through crafter diffusion
- [[the-architect]] — original commissioner
- [[ball]] — childhood resident; decrepit presence on return visits; wanted poster here
- [[four-squared]] — coalesced and broke out from the dueling courts
- [[outpost-system]] — one of twelve monthly outposts
- [[floor-01-red]] — host floor
- [[anchor-suits]] — players arrived here protected by them
- [[nonite]] — site of the liquefied-nonite time-distillation incident

## Open Questions
- Are there other surviving monthly outposts on Floor 1, and who or what occupies them?
- Is Valus's distributed presence in the systems still active enough to affect the space?
- Is the magistrate/arrival function still usable?
- What happened to the automaton staff after Four-Squared?

## Key Takeaways
- July is a survivor of a twelve-outpost system. Most are gone.
- Valus's retrofit is still there — chrome-white, empty, waiting.
- Ball's decrepit state on the players' return is the clearest diagnostic of how far he's fallen.

# The Spire

## Summary
An ancient staircase running through all seven floors of [[the-house]]. Predates the current floor power structures. A utilitarian transit tool from an era before entities could will themselves between floors or build their own gates. Mostly forgotten. The players stumbled upon it.

## Established
- **Age**: Ancient — built before current floor controllers existed, before gates and willed transit were common. From an era when the Spire was the primary way to move between floors.
- **The Architect** used it — not out of necessity but appreciation for its simplicity.
- **Form**: A literal staircase, large. From outside it resolves into a long upward spiral. Each floor's section is visually and atmospherically distinct — the Spire takes on the character of each floor as it passes through.
- **Entry/exit points**: Breaks in the staircase at each floor that deposit travelers at specific tactical or notable locations on that floor — not random exits but meaningful ones.
- **Fixed vs. variable exits**: Most exits are fixed. Some have been redirected over time by individuals who knew enough to manipulate them for their own purposes. The full current map is unknown.
- **Current status**: Largely forgotten. Not controlled by any faction. The majority of House inhabitants have never used it or know it exists. History of unknown manipulation baked in.
- Players found it by stumbling upon it — not by seeking it.

## Relationships
- [[the-house]] — internal structure spanning all floors
- [[the-architect]] — used it; may have built it
- [[the-scaffolding]] — sibling sub-realm; both are House infrastructure beyond the floor sequence
- [[floor-01-red]] — connects here (and to all other floors)

## Open Questions
- Who built the Spire — the Architect, or something older?
- Which exits have been manipulated, and by whom?
- Does the Spire have a top and bottom, or does it loop?
- Are there exits that lead somewhere other than a floor?

## Key Takeaways
- Predates current power structures. The old way in.
- Most exits fixed; some moved. Full map unknown.
- Almost nobody knows it exists.

# The Great Calamity

## Summary
The name given to [[caesar]]'s conquest and reshaping of [[floor-01-red]]. Not a natural disaster — a deliberate act of takeover that displaced the original Floor 1 reality into a pocket realm and imposed Caesar's cruelty-scape in its place. The soul storm was created as part of this reshaping. Ball disappeared in its aftermath.

## Established
- **What it was**: Caesar's conquest of Floor 1. The "Calamity" is the name applied to the whole conquest era — not a single moment but a period.
- **The soul storm**: Created by Caesar as part of the reshaping — not pre-existing. Functions as the boundary of his imposed reality, containing what people perceive as the floor and making it feel finite. What lies beyond the storm is unknown.
- **The original Floor 1**: Displaced into Caesar's controlled pocket realm. It still exists — preserved, inaccessible under normal circumstances. See [[pre-caesar-floor-1]].
- **Ball's disappearance**: Ball vanished in the aftermath. The stranding of the early recruiters may be directly connected to this event.
- **Valus's testimony**: Valus witnessed the aftermath; his knowledge of what actually happened is limited by how much he had already lost himself by then.
- **Common knowledge**: Most inhabitants of Floor 1 know the Calamity as a vague ancient catastrophe. Caesar's direct role is not common knowledge.

## Relationships
- [[caesar]] — cause; his conquest is the Calamity
- [[floor-01-red]] — the floor reshaped by it
- [[ball]] — disappeared in its aftermath
- [[valus]] — surviving witness; limited knowledge
- [[pre-caesar-floor-1]] — what was displaced
- [[floor-extension]] — the canonical example of this mechanic

## Open Questions
- Who held Floor 1 before Caesar, and what happened to them?
- What exactly happened to the early recruiters — was the stranding a direct result of the Calamity?
- Is Ball's origin tragedy (the first recruitment run going wrong) the same event as the Calamity, or adjacent to it?
- Does Caesar's death change anything about the soul storm or the displaced pocket realm?

## Key Takeaways
- The Calamity is Caesar's conquest, not a natural event. The soul storm is his creation.
- The original Floor 1 still exists, locked in his pocket realm.
- Most people on Floor 1 don't know Caesar caused it.

# House Mirroring

## Summary
The House deliberately mirrors real-world measurement and organizational systems as structural analogies — not decoratively but functionally. Clocks, calendars, card suits, solstice periods, and other cyclical or ranked systems from the external world have corresponding structures inside the House with real power attached. These mirrors serve as embedded clues to how the House works.

## Established
- Confirmed mirrored systems: calendar months (the outpost system), clock time units (temporal hierarchy), numerology broadly, card suits, solstice/seasonal periods.
- The mirrors are structural — they carry actual organizational power, not just aesthetic resemblance.
- Understanding real-world systems gives insight into the House's systems — the parallels are intentional and legible to those who know to look.
- The number of floors (7) aligns with ROYGBIV. The number of outposts (12) aligns with calendar months. The floor power structure (Hours/Minutes/Seconds) aligns with clock hierarchy. These are not coincidences.

## Details
- The Architect appears to have built the mirroring in deliberately — whether as a design philosophy, a message, or a functional constraint is unknown.
- Players from outside the House are uniquely positioned to recognize these mirrors; House-native entities may not perceive them as mirrors at all — just as the way things are.

## Relationships
- [[the-architect]] — likely designer of the mirroring principle
- [[temporal-hierarchy]] — clock-mirror manifestation
- [[outpost-system]] — calendar-mirror manifestation
- [[the-house]] — foundational principle of the House's structure

## Open Questions
- Are all real-world measurement/organizational systems mirrored, or only specific ones?
- Who designed the mirroring — the Architect, or is it an emergent property of the House's relationship to outside reality?
- Are the mirrors perfect, or do they have intentional distortions?
- Do the mirrors go both ways — does the House's structure affect its real-world analogs?

## Key Takeaways
- The House mirrors external organizational systems as functional analogies with real power.
- Players can use knowledge of real-world systems as a map for the House.
- The mirrors are a design feature, not a coincidence.

# Temporal Hierarchy

## Summary
A ranked lieutenant structure present on every floor with a controlling entity. The controller designates chosen individuals as **Hours**, **Minutes**, and **Seconds** — each imbued with the power of the floor through that title. The structure mirrors the real-world clock hierarchy and reflects [[house-mirroring]]. Hours are the most powerful; Seconds the least of the three.

## Established
- Every controlled floor has this structure beneath its controlling entity.
- Three ranks: **Hours** (highest), **Minutes** (mid), **Seconds** (lowest of the three).
- Designation is by the floor controller — chosen individuals, imbued with a portion of the floor's power through the title.
- The imbuing is real and functional — the titles carry actual power, not just status.
- Under Caesar on Floor 1: his Hours, Minutes, and Seconds were the top agents of his cruelty-scape. Their identities are not yet established.
- The three-rank structure may extend further — other time units (days, months, years) may have corresponding roles at higher scales.
- Part of the [[house-mirroring]] system — the clock as organizational analogy made structural.

## Relationships
- [[caesar]] — exemplar; his Hours/Minutes/Seconds ran Floor 1
- [[floor-01-red]] — site of the canonical example
- [[house-mirroring]] — clock-mirror manifestation of the broader principle
- [[the-house]] — cross-floor mechanic present on every controlled floor

## Open Questions

- Do the ranks extend beyond three (days, weeks, months)?
- What happens to the title-holders when a floor changes controllers — do the imbued powers transfer, fade, or remain?
- Can someone hold a title on multiple floors simultaneously?
- Are there equivalent structures on uncontrolled floors?

## Key Takeaways
- Every controlled floor has Hours, Minutes, Seconds as its top three lieutenants.
- The titles are power — not ceremonial.
