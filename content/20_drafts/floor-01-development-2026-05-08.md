## 1. Thematic Anchor

**Canon confirms:** Floor 1 is currently Caesar's cruelty-scape — a pocket reality imposed over the original floor. Red = heat, urgency, hunger, the entrance threshold.

**The contradiction:** Floor 1 is about *the warmth that cannot be trusted.* Arrivals feel heat — literal and metaphorical — and mistake it for safety, welcome, life. But Caesar designed every degree of that warmth to consume them. The floor feels alive because it is feeding.

**Red as emotion:** Not rage. Red on Floor 1 is *desperate hunger* — the feeling of wanting something so badly it hollows you out. The Floor 1 born don't know what they're hungry for. They just know the hunger is them.

## 2. The Current Landscape (Caesar's Imposition)

Caesar reshaped Floor 1 as an extension of himself. The landscape IS cruelty — not decorated with it.

### The Radial Chasm

A single, massive chasm spirals from the outer edge of the floor toward the center. The outposts were built along its walls. The chasm is the only practical route inward. Travel is:
- **Down** (toward center, toward Caesar's mirror-kingdom) — the soul storm presses inward as you descend, ambient temperature rises, the air thickens.
- **Up** (toward the outer edge, toward potential exit) — increasingly exposed to the soul storm's direct fury. Few travel this direction willingly.

The chasm walls are striated with abandoned structures from before the Calamity, exposed by Caesar's reshaping — bone-white stone, broken windows, doorways that open onto nothing.

### The Surface Above

No one goes to the surface. The chasm is where everything lives. What exists above the chasm's rim is unknown — even to most long-term residents — because the soul storm scours it constantly. Some say the surface is still the original Floor 1, preserved impossibly above the storm line, visible only when the storm thins.

### The Soul Storm as Geography

The soul storm is not weather — it is the floor's atmosphere, its boundary, and its digestive system. Key mechanics:

- **Composition:** Dissolved entities — souls, memories, identities of those consumed by Floor 1. Combat casualties, failed arrivals, things that wandered too long.
- **The scream**: The storm is audible at all times as a low, constant pressure on the edge of hearing — not loud, but always there. Long-term residents stop noticing it. New arrivals think it's their own heartbeat.
- **Feeding cycles:** The storm surges when something significant dies on Floor 1 — the soul ripples outward and the storm feeds. After a surge, the storm quiets for a measurable period.
- **The quiet zones:** Places where the storm's sound drops to near-silence. Rare. Usually means the area is saturated with death already, or something living there is louder than the storm. Both possibilities are dangerous.
- **Visibility:** The sky is a permanent bruised red-black, lit from within by occasional discharges — not lightning, but the release of dissolved souls. These discharges illuminate the landscape in brief, hellish clarity.

### The Mirror-Kingdom (Caesar's Capital)

At the center of the chasm's spiral. Canon establishes:
- Mirrors channel the soul storm to power Caesar directly.
- Twelve sons — manifestations of malignant power — serve as solar heralds.

**Proposed texture:**
- A city of reflective surfaces — black glass, polished obsidian, still water. The mirrors are functional infrastructure, not decorative. Each major mirror is tuned to a specific frequency of the soul storm. Together they form a harmonic array that converts the storm's chaos into usable power.
- The twelve sons are each tied to a specific mirror-array segment. If a son dies or is removed, the corresponding mirror goes dark, and the array loses a harmonic. Caesar was visibly diminished by their loss.
- The throne room is the array's focal point — a hall of converging mirrors where Caesar sat at the intersection of all reflected power. Empty now.
- The mirror-kingdom is not abandoned (Caesar's death notwithstanding), but the power is dead. The mirrors still reflect — but they no longer channel. The city is preserved in a state of permanent dimming.

### The Periphery

Beyond the outpost system and the chasm walls, between the last outpost and the outer edge: the *ash plains.* What happens when the soul storm scours a region for millennia. Fine grey ash, ankle-to-waist deep. Remains of structures beneath. No outposts here — never were. Carrier creatures sometimes nest in the buried ruins.

## 3. Arrival and the First Moments

Floor 1 is the arrival floor. How that works matters for every character and player who enters the House.

### The Entry Points

Canon establishes twelve outposts as arrival/magistrate hubs. Most destroyed. But arrival doesn't require an outpost — the outposts were just infrastructure for processing arrivals.

**Possible entry mechanisms:**
- **The threshold breach:** Arriving from outside creates a localized rent in reality. These rents heal at different rates depending on where they occur. An arrival in the ash plains might leave a scar that lasts centuries. An arrival at a functional outpost heals in moments because the infrastructure seals it.
- **The soul storm rejects you:** Arrivals enter the floor from *inside* the soul storm — they materialize in its outer edge and fall inward. The storm strips away whatever "outside" still clings to them. This is what assimilation means physically: the storm takes your outside-ness, and what lands on the floor is what the House leaves you with.
- **Ball's method:** When Ball transported the players, he bypassed the storm entirely — a sign of his deep assimilation. He could open a door through it because he was already made of the same stuff.

### What Arrivals Experience

1. A moment of total sensory deprivation — no color, no sound, no temperature.
2. The scream of the soul Storm — not loud yet, but recognizable as the background of all sound to come.
3. The heat — Floor 1 is warm. Not hostile warmth, not pleasant warmth. The warmth of something living nearby. The warmth of a body that is not yours.
4. The red — the color asserts itself gradually, as if the floor is remembering what it looks like.
5. The hunger — subtle at first. A craving for something undefined. It takes time (days, weeks) for arrivals to recognize that the craving is the floor itself, and that indulging it is how the floor keeps you.

## 4. The Outpost Network — Current State

Canon: Twelve outposts, one per month. July survives. Caesar destroyed some; attrition took others.

### Proposed Survivors

An honest accounting — not committing to all, but proposing a framework:

| Outpost | Status | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| January | Destroyed (Caesar) | First to fall. Pinpoint precision — Caesar wanted to make a statement. |
| February | Destroyed (attrition) | Collapsed into the chasm during a storm surge. |
| March | Unknown | Cut off before the Calamity. No evidence of destruction or survival. Some say it simply moved. |
| April | Destroyed (Caesar) | Casualty of the war for the floor. |
| May | Ruins, partially inhabited | Something lives in the remains — not outpost staff, not friendly. |
| June | Destroyed (attrition) | Flooded. The chasm section here is periodically submerged in something that is not water. |
| July | Surviving, abandoned | Valus's outpost. Post-Four-Squared and post-Valus. |
| August | Unknown | No records. The month itself may be significant. |
| September | Destroyed (Caesar) | Casualty of the war. |
| October | Destroyed (attrition) | Simply stopped transmitting one day. By the time anyone reached it, empty. |
| November | Surviving, occupied | Someone or something runs it now. Not Architect-authorized. |
| December | Unknown | The last outpost. Furthest from the mirror-kingdom. May be outside Caesar's influence entirely. |

The "Unknown" outposts (March, August, December) are deliberate — hooks for future development. The partially-inhabited ruins (May) and unauthorized occupier (November) create active points of interest without overcommitting.

## 5. The Temporal Hierarchy (Caesar's Lieutenants)

Canon: Hours (highest), Minutes, Seconds — top three lieutenants.

### The Hours

Caesar's second-in-command. Name suggests a position of immense authority. The Hours would have been empowered to act in Caesar's absence, command the mirror-array in his stead, and coordinate the outpost system. Identity: unknown. Status after Caesar's death: unknown.

### The Minutes

Operational commander. Ran day-to-day enforcement across the floor. Coordinated the temporal hierarchy's reach. Identity: unknown.

### The Seconds

Ground-level enforcers — not one entity but a class. Seconds were imbued with floor power to execute the hierarchy's will directly. Multiple Seconds served under each outpost's jurisdiction. Most were probably destroyed during the Four-Squared crisis and its aftermath.

**Open question:** Did the lieutenants survive Caesar? Did they serve him willingly, or were they bound by the same cruelty that defines the floor? A loyal Hour holding the mirror-kingdom in Caesar's absence is a very different story from a released Hour who now serves their own agenda.

## 6. The Mythos Layer

### What the Floor Remembers

Floor 1 has had at least two realities: the original (lush, magical medieval forest), and Caesar's imposition (cruelty-scape). But the floor *remembers* its previous states. The memories surface in specific ways:

- **Phantom landscapes:** In the quiet zones of the soul storm, some travelers report seeing the original forest superimposed over the chasm — brief, translucent, unreachable. The floor dreaming of what it was.
- **The Old Things:** Entities that predate Caesar still exist in deep pockets of the floor — places Caesar's reshaping didn't reach or didn't bother with. They are not friendly to Caesar, but that doesn't mean they are friendly to arrivals.
- **The original controller:** Before Caesar, someone or something held Floor 1. Not the Architect — a floor-level authority, akin to what Caesar became. Who they were, whether they survived the Calamity, and whether they are still present in the displaced pocket realm: all open.

### What the Floor Tells Itself

Every floor has a lie (per the toolkit). Floor 1's lie: *"The hunger is yours."*

Everything about Floor 1 — the warmth, the craving, the slow erosion of identity — convinces inhabitants that the hunger is personal. That it came with them from outside, or that it's who they are. But the hunger is the floor. It is Caesar's legacy, still operating after his death. Admitting that the hunger is external means admitting that the floor can be refused — and most inhabitants are too far gone to afford that admission.

### Chimerakind and the Deeper Mythos

Chimerakind unknowingly worshipped Caesar through his scattered organs. But the organs were scattered in the *displaced* pre-Caesar pocket realm. This means:
- Caesar was active across both realities — the imposed cruelty-scape and the displaced original. He conquered the surface but also seeded the depth.
- The organ-children (byproduct deities) exist in both realms by implication — some loose on the main floor now, some still in the pocket realm, some carried by players in ways they might not fully understand.
- The children-who-never-knew-the-father are now fatherless in a more permanent way.

## 7. What's at Stake Now (Post-Caesar)

The player storyline endpoint: Caesar is dead. The questions that creates:

1. **Does the cruelty-scape persist?** Caesar's death removed the active will maintaining the pocket realm, but the realm was imposed over millennia — it may not collapse immediately, or ever. It may simply become *unowned territory.*
2. **The soul storm without Caesar:** The storm was his creation and his power source. Does it dissipate? Go feral? Seek a new anchor?
3. **Power vacuum:** The Hours, surviving outposts, independent factions, Nero, the WIGHT, Four-Squared in the scaffolding — all may have claims or interests in a leaderless Floor 1.
4. **The displaced original:** With Caesar dead, can the original Floor 1 reintegrate? Does it want to? Is there anyone left there to ask?

- Who/what are the Hours and the Minutes?
- What is the surface above the chasm rim?
- Which unknown outposts (March, August, December) survive and how?
- Who occupies November?
- Does the soul storm persist or decay without Caesar?
- Are Caesar's twelve sons still active — and are they threats, neutrals, or potential allies?

- How does arrival work mechanically, and can it be controlled or disrupted?
- What is the relationship between Floor 1 entry and the Scaffolding?
