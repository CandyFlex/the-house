# Hunger's Edge
*Weapon (scimitar), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Hunger's Edge was forged from the fang of a blood-drinking cosmic entity — a World-Thirst that once drained entire planets dry, leaving them as desiccated husks spinning lifeless through the void. The entity was killed by a coalition of star-gods, but one of its teeth was caught in the killing blow and fell through the planes, landing in a mortal forge at the exact moment a smith was quenching a blade of black iron. The tooth melted into the metal, and the resulting scimitar has been hungry ever since.

The blade is serrated like a predator's tooth, dark iron veined with red lines that pulse faintly — slowly when the blade is fed, rapidly when it has been denied. The hilt is wrapped in something that resembles dried tendon, and the pommel is a smaller, backward-curving fang that occasionally twitches. The blade weeps a thin, blood-red fluid when hungry — not blood, but an anticoagulant enzyme that the World-Thirst used to keep its victims bleeding.

The consciousness within Hunger's Edge is simple and direct: it is hunger. Not hunger for power or vengeance or purpose — just hunger, the pure biological imperative to consume. It communicates through craving: a tightening in the wielder's stomach when a suitable target is near, a warm satisfaction when blood is drawn, a painful cramping when the blade has gone too long without feeding. It does not judge its wielder's morals. It does not care what the blood is from — only that it comes regularly. It will serve anyone who feeds it, and it will punish anyone who starves it. It is not malevolent. It is just hungry. You would be too, if you had gone this long without eating.

## Sealed State

Hunger's Edge is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **1d6 necrotic damage** — the blade drinks, and the wound does not close properly. Blood continues to weep from the injury, and the target takes **1d4 poison damage** at the start of its next turn as the blade's anticoagulant enzymes linger in the wound.

Once per turn when you hit a creature with Hunger's Edge, you regain hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt by the bonus dice — the blade drinks life from your enemy and shares it with you. This is not generosity. It is a symbiote's practicality: a dead wielder cannot feed it.

The blade demands sustenance. If you have not dealt damage with Hunger's Edge to a living creature within the past 24 hours, the blade turns its hunger on you. At the start of each hour after the 24-hour mark has passed, you take **1d4 necrotic damage** that cannot be reduced or prevented. This damage continues every hour until you feed the blade — by hitting a living creature with it — or until you unattune. The blade is not punishing you. It is surviving.

Additionally, you have advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks to track wounded creatures. The blade can taste blood in the air from up to 300 feet away, and it shares that taste with you — a faint coppery sensation on your tongue that grows stronger as you approach the source.

## Awakened State

Feed the blade the blood of fifty different creatures, and it will whisper the true name between one heartbeat and the next. As a bonus action, you speak the name — a word in the World-Thirst's language, a wet, guttural sound that tastes of iron in your mouth as you say it. The blade drinks the word itself and grows.

The scimitar extends — the serrated edge unfolds forward and downward like a predator's jaw unhinging, the metal stretching impossibly. The blade gains **reach of 10 feet** as its physical length increases to nearly five feet. The red veins pulse violently, visibly surging with stolen blood. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the necrotic bonus damage increases to **3d6**, and the poison linger damage increases to **1d6**. Additionally, every time you hit a creature with the awakened blade, the target begins to **Bleed** — a stacking condition unique to Hunger's Edge. A bleeding creature takes **1d4 necrotic damage** at the start of each of its turns. This damage stacks: the second hit adds another 1d4, the third another 1d4, to a maximum of **3d4 necrotic damage** per turn. A creature remains bleeding until it receives magical healing, succeeds on a DC 15 Wisdom (Medicine) check as an action, or the awakening ends. You regain hit points equal to half the total necrotic damage dealt to bleeding creatures at the start of your turn — the blade shares its feast.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can sweep the extended blade in a wide arc and **Drink Deeply** — each bleeding creature within 20 feet of you takes **3d6 necrotic damage** as the blade exerts a sudden pull on every wound it has opened. You regain hit points equal to half the total damage dealt by this effect.

## The True Name

The true name of Hunger's Edge is learned not through study or meditation but through feeding. The blade must drink the blood of **fifty different creatures** — each one distinct, not simply fifty goblins from the same warren. The blade keeps count. After each feeding, the wielder feels a pulse, a number forming in the back of their mind like a countdown. When the fiftieth creature's blood is drawn, the name crystallizes — a single word in the World-Thirst's dead language, delivered directly into the wielder's brain through the hilt. It is not a word that can be written down. It is a word that can only be spoken while tasting blood. The blade will not tell you this requirement. It assumes you will figure it out. Hunger teaches patience.
