# Vow-Keeper
*Weapon (longsword), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Vow-Keeper was not forged from metal. It was forged from the literal concept of a broken oath — extracted from the space between what was promised and what was done, the negative pressure that exists whenever a promise is violated. The forging event was a mass betrayal: a king who swore to protect his people and then sold them to slavers, a mother who promised her child safety and then locked the door and walked away, a god who vowed to answer prayers and then went silent. Three oaths, broken in the same moment across three different planes, created a resonance that someone — something — was able to catch and compress into physical form. The resulting blade is a longsword of silvery-grey metal, impossibly straight — its cross-section is a perfect rectangle, its edges parallel to a tolerance that physics should not allow. The blade reflects not the face of the person holding it, but the face of whoever last broke an oath in its presence, their expression frozen in the moment of the betrayal.

The reflection changes. Sometimes it shows a stranger — someone across the room who just lied to a friend. Sometimes it shows the wielder. The blade does not judge — at least, not in words. But being reflected in Vow-Keeper's surface is an uncomfortable experience, because the reflection is always slightly more honest than the reality. A liar's reflection shows their mouth full of ash. An oathbreaker's reflection shows their hands stained red.

The consciousness within Vow-Keeper is obsessed with promises. It remembers every promise ever made within 30 feet of it — the words, the speaker, the recipient, whether it was kept or broken. It does not communicate through speech or sensation. It communicates through the blade's reflection: when you are about to break a promise, your own reflection turns away from you. You are looking at the back of your own head. The blade favors wielders who keep their word — not honest people necessarily, but people who do what they said they would do, even when it costs them. It dislikes politicians, merchants who misrepresent their wares, and anyone who says "I'll be there in five minutes" when they haven't left the house yet.

## Sealed State

Vow-Keeper is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **2d6 force damage** — the binding energy of an oath kept, weaponized into a cutting edge.

When you make an **opportunity attack** with Vow-Keeper, the attack is made with **advantage** and deals an additional **3d6 force damage** (on top of the normal bonus damage, for a total of 5d6 force on a hit). A creature that tries to leave your reach without permission is breaking an unspoken agreement — the assumption that combatants will face each other — and the blade punishes breaches of protocol with binding light.

You always know when a creature within **30 feet** of you breaks a promise. You do not know the content of the promise or who it was made to — but the blade's reflection shifts, showing you the face of the oathbreaker as they appeared at the moment of betrayal. This is not divination. It is memory. The blade witnessed the promise being made, and it witnesses the promise being broken, and it shares what it saw.

While holding Vow-Keeper, you have advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks, and you cannot be surprised by any creature whose face you have seen — the blade has already shown you the moment they decided to betray you, even if that moment hasn't happened yet. Additionally, you cannot intentionally break a promise while attuned to the blade. If you attempt to speak a deliberate falsehood about something you have promised to do, you must succeed on a **DC 15 Charisma saving throw** or be physically unable to form the words. The blade will not be party to a lie. If you feel you absolutely must break your word, you can attempt a DC 20 Charisma saving throw to override the blade's objection — but the blade will remember, and its reflection will show your face for a full month afterward.

## Awakened State

The true name of Vow-Keeper is the exact words of a promise the wielder intends to keep until death — not a promise the wielder has already made, but one they are making now, in this moment, with the blade as witness. The promise must be something you mean, something you would die rather than break. The words must be your own, not scripted or borrowed. You must speak them while drawing the blade's edge across your own palm, an act that deals **1d4 slashing damage** and leaves a scar that will never fully fade.

As you speak, the blade drinks your blood — and if the promise is genuine, the blood ignites. Silver-white fire races up the edge, and the blade's reflection clears for the first time, showing nothing — pure, empty silver — because in this moment, there is no lie in you to reflect. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, your opportunity attacks with Vow-Keeper gain the **Oathbound Reach** — the range of your opportunity attacks becomes equal to your **movement speed**. When a creature provokes an opportunity attack from you, you can choose to teleport to any unoccupied space within 5 feet of the creature and make the attack there, appearing in a flash of binding silver light. The strike delivers 5d6 force damage in addition to the weapon's normal damage.

The blade's awareness of betrayal intensifies. The first time each round that a creature within **30 feet** of you breaks a promise, the oathbreaker's own reflection turns against it — it takes **4d6 radiant damage** as the light of its own broken word sears it from within. The creature knows, instantly and intuitively, that Vow-Keeper is the source. It also knows, instantly and intuitively, exactly what promise it broke. The blade does not allow convenient forgetfulness.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, you can designate one creature within 30 feet as the target of **Oath-Sight** — you learn whether that creature has ever broken a promise to you, and if so, the exact words of the promise and the exact moment it was broken. The knowledge arrives as a complete sensory memory, as if you yourself had witnessed the betrayal.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can make a single melee attack against a creature within your reach (including your Oathbound Reach) and declare a **Binding Oath** — a single-sentence command such as "You will not harm my allies," "You will not move from this spot," or "You will speak only truth." The attack is made with advantage. On a hit, the target must make a **DC 19 Charisma saving throw**. On a failure, the creature is compelled to follow the oath for 1 minute. If it violates the terms, it takes **8d10 radiant damage** and the Binding Oath ends. The creature knows the terms and the penalty. The blade will enforce what your words promised.

## The True Name

The true name of Vow-Keeper is not a single word — it is the text of a promise the wielder intends to keep until death, spoken for the first time aloud while holding the blade. The promise cannot be trivial ("I promise to eat breakfast") or already kept ("I promise I used to be a soldier"). It must be a genuine, weight-bearing vow — something that will cost you, something that matters, something that could define the rest of your life. The wielder must draw the blade across their palm while speaking the promise, and the blade will judge the words. If the promise is genuine, the name is accepted and the blade awakens. If the promise is hollow or performative, the blade remains inert, and the cut on your palm stings with the particular shame of being caught lying to yourself.
