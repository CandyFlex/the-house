# Dawn-Sever
*Weapon (longsword), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Dawn-Sever was forged at the instant of dawn on the peak of a mountain that no longer exists — a mountain so tall that the sun rose over the world's curve before it rose anywhere else, and the first light of day touched only that peak for a full three minutes before reaching the valleys below. The smith was the light itself, focused through a lens of crystalized atmosphere, and the metal was a bar of pure orichalcum that had been held in the peak's shadow for ten thousand years, waiting for a dawn that would never touch it. When the light finally struck the metal, it did not heat it. It transformed it — not melting, but converting the orichalcum into captured sunlight, a blade of solid radiance that is visible even with closed eyes.

The blade is not gold or white or yellow. It is the color of the sky at the exact moment the sun's disc first breaks the horizon — a pale, piercing rose-gold that seems to exist simultaneously as a color and as a source of illumination. The flat of the blade shows a permanent after-image of the sun's corona at the moment of forging: rings of light frozen in the metal like tree rings, each one marking a second of that first dawn. The edge is not sharp in the traditional sense — it does not cut. It separates. Matter that touches the edge is un-made, converted to light and warmth the way the first dawn erased the last shadows of night.

The consciousness within Dawn-Sever is the memory of that first dawn — not an intelligence that thinks or speaks, but a presence that *illuminates*. It does not communicate. It reveals. When you hold the blade, things that were hidden become visible — not just physical objects, but truths, intentions, the faintest trace of darkness that a creature carries in its heart. The blade favors wielders who bring light to dark places, who protect the dawn against the endless hunger of the night. It despises undead and fiends with a hatred so pure it is almost holy. It will not serve anyone who commands or consorts with undeath — the blade will simply refuse to be drawn, its light retreating into the metal, leaving only a cold hilt and the scent of disappointment that smells faintly like a candle just blown out.

## Sealed State

Dawn-Sever is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. The blade's edge does not cut flesh — it annihilates the darkness in it. The weapon deals **radiant damage instead of slashing damage**. When you hit a creature, the attack deals an additional **2d6 radiant damage** as the first light of dawn scours the target's form.

When you hit an **undead or fiend** with Dawn-Sever, the creature is exposed to concentrated, weaponized dawn. It must succeed on a **DC 19 Charisma saving throw** or be **turned** for 1 minute, as if by a cleric of your character level using the Turn Undead channel divinity feature. The blade does not ask permission from any god. It *is* the dawn, and the dawn has never needed anyone's blessing to banish the night.

Dawn-Sever sheds **bright light in a 15-foot radius** and **dim light for an additional 15 feet**. This light is **true sunlight** — not merely bright, but the actual substance of dawn, capable of destroying vampires, disrupting the regeneration of trolls adapted to darkness, and suppressing the Sunlight Sensitivity of drow and similar creatures. You can suppress or rekindle this light as a bonus action.

While standing in direct sunlight — natural, unfiltered sunlight, not merely the blade's own glow — your speed increases by **10 feet**, and you have advantage on saving throws against effects originating from undead or fiends. The blade draws strength from the same source that created it, and when that source is overhead, you are connected to something vast and ancient and utterly inimical to the things that lurk in darkness.

## Awakened State

The true name is the last word spoken by the sun before it set on the day Dawn-Sever was forged. To speak it, you must stand at the exact geographic location of the forging — the coordinates of a mountain that no longer exists — at the exact moment of dawn. If you do, the blade will speak one word directly into your mind: a word in a language that predates humanoid speech, the language of celestial bodies and orbital mechanics and fusion reactions. It will burn itself into your memory, and for the rest of your life, you will be able to speak it — but only at dawn.

Speak the name as an action, and the blade becomes a beam. The physical metal of the longsword dissolves into pure dawn-light — a column of rose-gold radiance the length of a blade, without weight or substance, passing through matter that is not actively trying to stop it. The blade's form is visible as a bar of light too bright to look at directly. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, Dawn-Sever's reach extends to **15 feet** as the beam of light stretches toward darkness like something eager. Attacks made with the blade **ignore half cover and three-quarters cover** — the blade is light, and light does not care about obstacles. The radiant bonus damage increases to **4d6**.

The turning effect intensifies. When you hit an undead or fiend while the blade is awakened, the creature makes its Charisma saving throw against the turning effect at **disadvantage**. Additionally, any undead creature that starts its turn within **10 feet** of you takes radiant damage equal to **your character level** — the proximity of the dawn is a form of ongoing exorcism.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, the blade's light pulses — every creature of your choice within the blade's bright light radius must make a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw** or be **blinded** until the start of your next turn. The light is not angry. It is simply what the dawn does: it blinds anyone who has been in darkness too long.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can sweep Dawn-Sever in a line and release **First Light** — a **60-foot line of true dawn 5 feet wide**. Each creature in the line must make a **DC 19 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **10d10 radiant damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also blinded for 1 minute, repeating the save at the end of each of its turns to end the blindness. Undead and fiends that fail the saving throw take **15d10 radiant damage** instead of 10d10. The line persists until the start of your next turn as a beam of stationary dawn-light — any creature that enters the line's area for the first time on a turn or ends its turn there takes 5d10 radiant damage.

## The True Name

The true name of Dawn-Sever is the last word spoken by the sun before it set on the day of the blade's forging — a single word in the language of celestial bodies, the language of fusion and orbital mechanics and the slow death of hydrogen. To learn it, the wielder must travel to the exact geographic location where the forging occurred — a mountain peak that no longer exists, its coordinates known only through the astronomical markings on the blade's fuller. There, at the exact moment of dawn, the wielder must stand in the first light and hold the blade to the rising sun. The blade will speak one word into the wielder's mind — and the wielder must survive the speaking, because the word is also a weapon. The first time the name is heard, the wielder must make a **DC 20 Constitution saving throw**, taking 10d10 radiant damage on a failure and half as much on a success. The body is not built to contain a word spoken by the sun. If you survive, the name is yours.
