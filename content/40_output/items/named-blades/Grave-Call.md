# Grave-Call
*Weapon (maul), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Grave-Call was forged from the bell of a chapel that burned with its congregation inside — not an accident, but a murder, a warlord sealing the doors and setting the roof alight to send a message to a god that never answered. The bell, heated beyond tolerance, fell from its tower and struck the stone floor at the exact moment the last congregant drew their final breath. Metal and death-rattle fused in that instant — the bell's bronze absorbed the sound of a hundred souls departing at once, and the resulting metal, when cooled by the blood of the dead pooling beneath it, had become something new. A smith who had been waiting for exactly this moment — a servant of the god of graves, who had foreseen the massacre and done nothing — shaped the bell-metal into a maul. The god blessed it. The smith did not. He walked into the sea the moment the maul was complete, and his body was never found.

The maul's head is a bell — a real bell, with a visible clapper suspended in a dark hollow at its center. When the maul strikes anything, it tolls: a deep, resonant note that seems to come from much farther away than the point of impact. The sound lingers too long — ten, fifteen seconds — and anyone who hears it feels a chill that has nothing to do with temperature. The haft is dark oak banded with tarnished silver, and the grip is wrapped in what appears to be black velvet ribbon, perpetually cold to the touch.

The consciousness within Grave-Call is not a single entity but a **chorus** — the hundred souls who died in the chapel, their voices layered into one tolling note that speaks in unison. They do not want vengeance. Vengeance would require anger, and they have been dead too long for anger. They want acknowledgment. They want someone to remember that they existed, that they died, that their deaths mattered to someone. They communicate through the bell's resonance — when the wielder does something the chorus approves of, the bell rings a single clear note. When the wielder does something the chorus opposes, the bell is silent even on impact — a dead, hollow thud, no ring at all. The chorus favors wielders who mourn, who remember, who visit graveyards and speak the names of the dead aloud. It despises necromancers, anyone who raises the dead without permission, and anyone who kills without acknowledging the weight of what they have ended. If you try to raise a creature the maul has killed, the chorus will scream — a sound that bypasses the ears and burrows directly into the soul.

## Sealed State

Grave-Call is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the bell tolls — and the target hears its own death in the resonance. The attack deals an additional **2d6 psychic damage** as the bell's frequency attunes to the target's mortality and plays it back as pain. The target must also make a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw** or be **frightened** of you until the end of your next turn. It has heard the bell that will ring for its passing, and it knows that bell is *yours*.

When a creature is reduced to 0 hit points by Grave-Call, the bell claims it. The creature cannot be resurrected by any means short of a ***wish*** spell — not *revivify*, not *raise dead*, not *resurrection*, not even *true resurrection*. The bell tolls once, and the soul is marked as belonging to the grave. This is not malice. It is the chorus adding one more voice to the choir.

While holding Grave-Call, you have advantage on death saving throws. The chorus is not ready for your voice yet, and while you remain useful, they will hold the grave at bay. Additionally, you always know when a creature within 60 feet of you is below half its hit point maximum — the bell tolls softly, a single note in your mind, for each one.

## Awakened State

The true name of Grave-Call is a funeral dirge — a song of mourning and farewell, specific to the culture and beliefs of the person you loved most who died of natural causes. You must attend the natural death (not combat, not accident, but the slow ending of age or illness or simple weariness) of someone you genuinely love, while holding the maul. At the moment of death, the bell will ring once — a perfect, clear note — and the dirge will form in your mind: a song you have never heard but suddenly know by heart, as if you have been singing it your whole life.

Speak the dirge aloud as an action, and the bell begins to toll continuously. The clapper swings of its own accord, and with each toll, the sound deepens — lower, heavier, more resonant — until it vibrates in the bones of everyone who can hear it. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the psychic bonus damage increases to **3d6**, and the maul deals an additional **2d6 necrotic damage** as the bell's tolling accelerates the decay of living tissue. The frightened condition now lasts until the target succeeds on a DC 19 Wisdom saving throw at the end of its turn, and a creature frightened of you in this way cannot take reactions or bonus actions — it is too consumed with the sound of its own approaching end.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, the **Death-Knell** radiates outward from you. Every creature of your choice that can hear and is currently frightened of you takes **3d8 psychic damage** as the bell's resonance intensifies inside their mind — the tolling growing louder and louder, the note of their personal funeral bell drowning out all other thought. The radius of this effect starts at **30 feet** on the first round, then expands by 10 feet each subsequent round (40, 50, 60) to a maximum of 60 feet. Creatures that are deafened are immune to this damage.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can raise the maul and bring it down against the air itself, releasing the chorus's voice in a **Funeral Toll** — a 60-foot-radius pulse of absolute, undeniable mortality. Each creature of your choice in the area must make a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw**. A creature at or below half its hit point maximum that fails the save drops to **0 hit points** and is stable — unconscious but not dying, the bell having rung for them and found them ready. A creature above half its hit point maximum that fails the save takes **8d10 psychic damage** and is frightened of you for 1 minute (no repeat save). On a successful save, a creature takes half damage and is not frightened. A creature reduced to 0 hit points by Funeral Toll — or already at 0 hit points — is claimed by the bell and cannot be resurrected by anything short of *wish*.

## The True Name

The true name of Grave-Call is a funeral dirge — not a word, but a song, a melody of mourning that is unique to the person you loved most who died a natural death. To learn it, you must attend the deathbed of someone you genuinely love — not an ally in combat, not a stranger you pity, but someone whose death will leave a hole in your life. You must hold the maul throughout their final hours. At the moment of death, the bell rings of its own accord, and the dirge is sung into your memory by a hundred dead voices. You will never forget it. You will never want to sing it again. But when you do — when you need the bell to toll for someone else — the dirge will rise in your throat unbidden, and the chorus will sing with you.
