# Salt-Heart
*Weapon (scimitar), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Salt-Heart was forged at the bottom of a sea that no longer exists — an ocean that evaporated over the course of a hundred thousand years, its waters stolen by a dying sun and its bed left as a salt flat that stretches beyond the horizon in every direction. At the exact center of that vanished sea, where the last drop of water fell from the last dying wave, the salt crystallized around a single grain of silica — the memory of a beach that had not seen the sky in a million years. That crystal, grown over millennia of desiccation and silence, became the blade of Salt-Heart: a scimitar of white salt that never dissolves, never melts, never weeps moisture of any kind. It is perpetually encrusted with fresh salt crystals that grow and crumble in a slow cycle, like frost forming on a window in reverse — inward, consuming rather than decorating.

The blade is rough to the touch, the texture of dried salt flats, and it leaves a white residue on anything it touches. The hilt is wrapped in what looks like dried kelp, brittle but unbreakable. Holding Salt-Heart, you feel a constant, low-grade thirst — not debilitating, but present, a reminder that water exists somewhere and you do not have it. The air around the blade is noticeably drier; paper near it crinkles, flowers wilt within hours, and liquids left in open containers within 5 feet evaporate at twice their normal rate.

The consciousness within Salt-Heart is the memory of the vanished sea — patient, patient beyond comprehension, waiting for the water to return the way a desert waits for rain that will never fall. It does not speak. It thirsts. It communicates through dehydration: when the wielder is in danger, their mouth goes dry, their throat constricts, their body sends the ancient signal — *water, find water* — and the wielder learns to interpret this as the blade's warning. It favors wielders who understand scarcity, who have gone without, who have been thirsty and learned that thirst is a teacher. It dislikes waste, excess, and people who leave taps running. It will not serve anyone who desecrates fresh water — damming rivers without need, poisoning wells, dumping waste into clean sources. The sea is gone. The blade will not help anyone make another one disappear.

## Sealed State

Salt-Heart is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the blade's salt crystals lodge in the wound and the attack deals an additional **1d8 cold damage** and **1d8 necrotic damage** — the cold of water denied and the necrosis of tissue left to dry rather than heal.

When you hit a creature with Salt-Heart, the salt begins its work. The target must succeed on a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw** or begin **desiccating**. While desiccating, the creature has disadvantage on Strength and Constitution ability checks, and its speed is reduced by **10 feet**. The salt draws moisture from the creature's tissues, leaving muscles rigid and joints stiff. This condition persists until the creature consumes at least **1 gallon of water** (requiring an action if water is available) or until it receives magical healing that restores at least 10 hit points. Creatures that do not need to drink water — constructs, most undead, many elementals — are immune to desiccation. The blade cannot steal what does not exist.

While holding Salt-Heart, you are immune to the effects of extreme heat (as the ***endure elements*** spell for hot environments) and you do not suffer levels of exhaustion from thirst. The blade draws ambient moisture to you — not enough to quench your thirst, but enough to keep you alive in conditions that would kill anyone else. Additionally, while you are in a desert, salt flat, or similarly arid environment, the blade's cold and necrotic bonus damage each increase to **2d8** — Salt-Heart is home, and home strengthens it.

## Awakened State

Cross a salt flat, desert, or dried ocean bed alone with nothing but the blade. Walk until your lips crack and your tongue swells and you cannot remember what water tastes like. At the point of maximum thirst — when your body is screaming and your mind is quiet and the world has narrowed to a single, shining need — the name will crystallize on your tongue: a word in the language of the vanished sea, a word that means *gone*.

Speak the name as an action, and Salt-Heart does not ignite or transform. It *spreads*. The salt crystals on the blade crackle and multiply, extending outward into the air in a growing storm of white particulate — a miniature salt flat that moves with the blade. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the blade's cold and necrotic bonus damage each increase to **2d8** (or **3d8** if already in an arid environment). The desiccation condition becomes more aggressive — when a target fails the Constitution saving throw, its speed reduction increases to **15 feet**, and it also gains vulnerability to cold damage until the desiccation ends. Dry things burn colder.

The true horror of the awakened blade is the **Salt-Storm Aura** — a 15-foot-radius field of desiccating particulate that moves with you. At the start of each of your turns, each creature of your choice within the aura must make a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw**. On a failure, the creature gains **one level of exhaustion** as moisture is ripped from its body. A creature can gain no more than 3 levels of exhaustion from this effect per awakening. Exhaustion levels gained from the Salt-Storm fade after 1 minute — but only if the creature consumes at least 1 gallon of water during that minute. Otherwise, the exhaustion persists and must be recovered through normal means (primarily long rests). Creatures that do not require water are immune to this exhaustion, but not to the aura's other effects.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can sweep the blade in a wide arc and conjure the **Mirage of Thirst** — a 30-foot-radius manifestation of the vanished sea's dying memory. Each creature of your choice in the area must make a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, the creature is convinced — utterly, desperately convinced — that it is dying of thirst. It must use its action on its next turn to attempt to drink (falling to the ground and searching for water if none is immediately available). Additionally, it takes **10d6 psychic damage** as its own body begins to mimic the symptoms of terminal dehydration — skin cracking, throat closing, mind fracturing into hallucination. On a success, a creature takes half as much damage and is not compelled. For 1 minute after this effect resolves, the area of the Mirage becomes lightly obscured by swirling salt — any creature that ends its turn in the area takes 1d6 cold damage.

## The True Name

The true name of Salt-Heart is a word in the language of the vanished sea — a language that no longer has speakers, that died with the last wave. To learn it, the wielder must cross a salt flat, a true desert, or the bed of a dried ocean — alone, with nothing but the blade. They must walk until thirst becomes the only thought in their mind, until their body has forgotten what water is, until the line between wanting and needing is erased entirely. At the point of maximum thirst — a threshold the blade recognizes — the name will crystallize on the wielder's tongue the way salt crystallizes on a drying shore: sudden, inevitable, and impossible to remove. The blade does not teach the name. It simply stops hiding it.
