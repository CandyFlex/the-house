# Rattle-Root
*Weapon (longsword), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Rattle-Root was not forged — it was **grown**, from a seed that fell from the World-Tree before the first mortal picked up a weapon. The seed landed in a primordial forest where metal did not exist, where the boundary between plant and animal was still being negotiated by evolution, and where the concept of "sword" had not yet been invented. The tree that grew from that seed shaped itself into a blade over millennia, responding to some ancient instinct — or perhaps some ancient command — that violence was coming, and the forest needed something that could fight back.

The result is a longsword made entirely of wood — but not dead wood. Rattle-Root's blade is living bark over a core of heartwood harder than iron, its surface rough and warm to the touch. Small leaves sprout from the crossguard, curling and uncurling in a slow rhythm like baby hands testing the air. In spring, tiny white flowers bloom along the fuller. In autumn, the leaves turn crimson and fall, only to regrow within days. The blade bleeds a thin green sap when wounded — not blood, but chlorophyll extruded through bark, proof that photosynthesis is still happening inside the sword.

The consciousness within Rattle-Root is ancient and slow. It thinks in seasons, not seconds. It communicates through growth — a leaf unfurling means approval, a leaf wilting means warning, a flower blooming means the blade is pleased. It does not understand haste or ambition or conquest. It understands sun, water, soil, and the long patience of things that grow. It favors wielders who respect the natural world — druids, rangers, farmers, anyone who has ever planted something and waited. It dislikes fire-users, deforesters, and anyone who kills things without eating them. To Rattle-Root, unnecessary death is a waste of good fertilizer. It will actively resist being used to destroy plant creatures or burn forests — the blade becomes heavy and dull, its edge retreating into the bark like a turtle pulling into its shell.

## Sealed State

Rattle-Root is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the blade's thorns dig deep, adding **1d4 piercing damage**. Additionally, the target must succeed on a **DC 17 Constitution saving throw** or tiny seeds burrow into the wound — an effect called **Rooting Seeds**. While seeded, the creature takes **1d4 piercing damage** at the start of each of its turns as roots spread beneath its skin. At the end of each of its turns, the creature repeats the saving throw, ending the effect on a success.

If a creature dies while affected by Rooting Seeds, the seeds complete their life cycle in seconds rather than seasons. An **Awakened Shrub** sprouts from the corpse, erupting from the chest cavity in a burst of green shoots and white flowers. The shrub is friendly to you and your allies, obeys your verbal commands (no action required), and acts on your initiative. It uses the statistics of an Awakened Shrub (as per the *awaken* spell), with the following changes: it has a number of hit points equal to your proficiency bonus × 10, and it can make a single attack on each of its turns — a thorny branch strike (+5 to hit, reach 5 feet, 1d6+2 piercing damage on a hit). It wilts and dies after 1 hour. You can have a number of shrubs active at once equal to your proficiency bonus.

While holding Rattle-Root, you have advantage on Intelligence (Nature) checks and Wisdom (Survival) checks made in forested terrain. The blade photosynthesizes — you need only half the normal amount of food and water while attuned to it, as the blade shares nutrients with you through your grip.

## Awakened State

Cultivate Rattle-Root in soil gathered from the forest where it was first grown — the blade must be planted, watered, and left to rest for one full cycle of the moon. When retrieved, it will have grown a single fruit on the crossguard: a small, green-gold apple. Eat the fruit, and the name of an extinct plant — the species Rattle-Root came from — will form in your mind. Speak that name as an action, and the blade blooms.

The sword erupts with growth — vines spiral down the blade, leaves unfurl in rapid sequence along the fuller, flowers burst open and release clouds of golden pollen. Within **30 feet** of you, the ground becomes **Verdant Surge**: an overgrown thicket of grasping vines and thorny undergrowth. The area is difficult terrain for creatures of your choice, and creatures of your choice that start their turn in the area must succeed on a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw** or take **1d6 piercing damage** from the thorns. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the Rooting Seeds piercing damage increases to **1d6+2 per turn**, and the save DC for the seeds rises to **18**. Additionally, at the start of each of your turns while you are in contact with natural ground (soil, grass, stone — not worked floors or artificial surfaces), you regain **5 hit points**. This is not magical healing — it is photosynthesis accelerated to combat speed, the blade converting sunlight and soil into cellular repair for its wielder. The regeneration increases to **10 hit points** if you are also in direct sunlight.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can target a creature you can see within 30 feet and command the Verdant Surge to attack. Vines erupt from the ground around the creature — it must make a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw** or be **restrained** by **Stranglethorn**. While restrained, the creature takes **3d6 piercing damage** at the start of each of its turns as the vines constrict. The creature can use its action to make a DC 17 Strength (Athletics) check, escaping on a success. Stranglethorn persists for the remainder of the awakening or until the creature escapes.

## The True Name

The true name of Rattle-Root is the scientific name of an extinct plant species — the very species that Rattle-Root itself belongs to, now dead in the wild and surviving only as this single living blade. The name is not in any modern language but in the druidic tongue of the primordial forest, a long compound word that describes the plant's shape, growth pattern, and the sound its seed-pods made in the wind. To learn the name, the wielder must cultivate the blade: bury it in soil gathered from old-growth forest, water it daily for one full lunar cycle, and then eat the fruit it produces. The fruit contains the name — and the genetic memory of the extinct species, which will play across the wielder's mind in a dream-vision of the primordial forest. The name will be the last thing the wielder hears before waking: the sound of seed-pods rattling in a wind that no longer blows.
