# Mind-Shard
*Weapon (dagger), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Mind-Shard was forged from the first thought that ever existed — not a thought in any mortal mind, but the original idea, the spark of consciousness that ignited at the beginning of everything and asked the first question. What that question was, no one knows — not even the blade remembers, because the blade is a fragment of the answer, not the question. It was forged during the reality-breach event that created Split-Whisper — when the dying astral leviathan's brain was torn in half, the split did not merely produce the twin shortswords. A third piece, smaller than the rest, detached and crystallized alone in the void between planes. It is a splinter of pure thought: a dagger that is invisible to anyone who cannot see invisible objects, and that leaves no physical wound when it strikes. Instead, it cuts memory.

The blade — if it can be called a blade — feels like cold glass that isn't there. Your hand knows it is holding something, but your eyes insist your hand is empty. The dagger leaves a faint afterimage in peripheral vision only, a shimmer like heat above summer asphalt, gone the moment you look directly at it. It weighs almost nothing. It makes no sound when it strikes. It is the perfect assassination tool — not because it kills, but because the victim may not remember being attacked at all.

The consciousness within Mind-Shard is a curator. It collects memories the way a museum collects artifacts — carefully, respectfully, with an archivist's obsessive attention to detail. It communicates through remembering: a memory suddenly surfacing, unbidden — not a warning or a message, just a fragment of the wielder's own past, offered as context for the present moment. The blade favors wielders who value knowledge, who understand that what you remember is who you are, who treat memories as precious rather than disposable. It does not dislike the forgetful — it pities them. But it actively despises anyone who destroys knowledge deliberately: book-burners, mind-wipers, anyone who would use the blade to erase rather than to collect.

## Sealed State

Mind-Shard is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. It deals **psychic damage instead of piercing damage** and leaves no physical wound — the target's flesh is unbroken, but their mind recoils from the impact as if struck. When you hit a creature, the attack deals an additional **2d6 psychic damage** as the blade scrapes against the target's memory-surface, dislodging fragments of self.

When you hit a creature with Mind-Shard, you can choose a **memory category** to target: a specific face, a specific name, a specific location, a skill proficiency, or a known language. The target must make a **DC 19 Intelligence saving throw**. On a failure, the target loses access to one memory from that category for **1 hour**. The exact memory lost is determined by the DM, but it should be relevant and disorienting. A creature that loses a skill proficiency has disadvantage on all ability checks using that skill for the duration. A creature that loses a language cannot speak, read, write, or understand that language for the duration. A creature that loses a face cannot recognize that person. When the effect ends, the memory returns — though the target may be left with an unsettling sense of having forgotten something important.

Mind-Shard is invisible — it is permanently under the effects of an ***invisibility*** spell that cannot be dispelled, suppressed, or bypassed except by effects that allow a creature to see invisible objects (such as *see invisibility* or *true seeing*). While attuned to the blade, you can see it clearly — to you, it is a shard of pale, translucent crystal, like frozen thought given shape. Additionally, you can use a bonus action to render the blade visible or invisible to all observers.

While holding Mind-Shard, your thoughts are protected. You have advantage on saving throws against effects that would read your thoughts, alter your memories, or impose madness. The blade guards your mind as jealously as it guards its own collection.

## Awakened State

The true name of Mind-Shard is the wielder's first memory — the earliest thing they can remember, the foundational experience upon which all later memory is built. To access it, the wielder must meditate in complete darkness and silence, pressing the dagger's point against their own temple. This does not deal damage — the blade cannot cut flesh — but it requires a **DC 15 Wisdom check** to maintain focus for 10 consecutive minutes. If the check fails, the wielder must begin again. On a success, the wielder relives their first memory in perfect, sensory detail — and at the heart of that memory, a word is embedded. The word is the name.

Speak the name as a bonus action, and the blade stops pretending to be a weapon. It becomes what it has always been: a shard of pure consciousness, a piece of the first thought, the thing that asked the first question and has been waiting ever since for an answer it is willing to accept. The blade's crystal form becomes visible even to those without magical sight — a jagged shard of pale blue-white light, humming at a frequency that makes teeth ache and eyes water. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the psychic bonus damage increases to **3d6**, and the blade's edge extends into the Ethereal Plane — you can **attack creatures on the Ethereal Plane as if they were on the Material Plane**, and vice versa. The blade now exists in both planes simultaneously, and its strikes bypass the planar boundary that would normally separate them.

The memory-loss effect becomes terrifying. When a creature fails the Intelligence saving throw, the memory loss is now **permanent** until the creature receives the benefits of a ***greater restoration*** spell or similar powerful magic. The blade does not borrow memories anymore. It takes them.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, you can choose a creature within 30 feet and attempt to read its surface thoughts — as per the ***detect thoughts*** spell, but requiring no saving throw for surface-level reading. The blade connects your consciousness to the target's, and what flows through is sensation and image and word-fragment, a torrent of self that you can parse at will.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can attempt a **Memory Theft** — the blade's ultimate function. Make a melee attack against a creature within your reach. On a hit, the target must make a **DC 19 Intelligence saving throw**. On a failure, you learn one specific memory of your choice from the target's life — you describe the general subject (their mother's face, their first kill, the location of their hidden treasure, the password to their stronghold), and the DM provides the memory in full sensory detail. The target forgets this memory permanently — it can only be restored by *greater restoration* or a *wish* spell. On a success, the target still takes the attack's normal damage plus an additional **4d6 psychic damage** from the psychic intrusion, but retains the memory. A creature that has no memories to steal (constructs with no consciousness, some undead) is immune to the memory-loss effect but not the damage.

## The True Name

The true name of Mind-Shard is hidden in the wielder's own past — specifically, in their first memory, the earliest thing they can consciously recall. The wielder must meditate while pressing the dagger's point to their temple, requiring a DC 15 Wisdom check to maintain the necessary focus for 10 uninterrupted minutes. During this meditation, the wielder will relive their first memory — not as they remember it, but as it actually happened, in perfect, unforgiving clarity. At the core of the memory, wrapped inside the first sensory impression of light or sound or touch, is a word — the blade's name, placed there at the moment the wielder first became conscious. The blade planted it there, in every sentient mind that has ever existed, waiting for someone to look inward far enough to find it. The name was always yours. You just forgot.
