# Spire-Kin
*Weapon (rapier), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Spire-Kin was forged from a splinter of a tower that touched the sky — a wizard's spire so tall that its uppermost chamber existed in the stratosphere, where the air was too thin to breathe and the stars were visible at noon. The tower was destroyed in a cataclysm (the wizard who built it had angered something that lived above the atmosphere), but a single shard of its pinnacle — a needle of stone and crystallized ambition — was caught by a passing storm and struck by lightning exactly seven times on its fall to earth. By the time it landed, it was no longer stone. It was a rapier, humming with ozone and the memory of thin air.

The blade is impossibly thin and impossibly straight — a needle of pale grey metal that seems to taper to infinity, its point too fine to be seen clearly with the naked eye. When held vertically, the blade hums at a frequency just below audible range. When pointed at the sky, tiny arcs of blue-white electricity crackle along its length. The guard is a ring of wrought iron that looks like a circlet of cloud, and the grip is wrapped in pale leather made from something that might be dragon wing membrane.

The consciousness within Spire-Kin is aspirational — it wants to go higher, always higher. It communicates through a sensation of lightness or heaviness in the wielder's legs: when you are standing on a hilltop or rooftop, the blade feels buoyant, eager, pulling upward. When you are underground or in a low place, it feels heavy with disappointment. It favors wielders who climb, who build, who reach — mountaineers, architects, anyone with the ambition to stand at the highest point and look down. It dislikes caves, dungeons, and basements. If you try to take Spire-Kin underground for too long, the blade will grow cold and inert, its lightning snuffed like a candle in a vacuum. The spire did not survive the cataclysm to be buried again.

## Sealed State

Spire-Kin is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **1d6 lightning damage** — a discharge of the static potential accumulated by existing at high altitude, released into the target's nervous system.

While holding Spire-Kin, you can cast ***feather fall*** at will, targeting only yourself and requiring no material components. The blade arrests your descent — not by magic in the traditional sense, but by extending a spire of invisible force downward from your feet, matching your falling speed to a gentle drift. You are also immune to falling damage while holding the blade. You do not land. You *arrive*.

Once per long rest, as a bonus action, you can activate **Vertical Step** — you teleport up to 60 feet straight up or straight down. You appear balanced on a shimmering **5-foot square platform of force energy** that extends beneath your feet, hovering in place as if you were standing on the very tip of an invisible spire. The platform lingers until the end of your next turn and can support your weight plus 500 pounds. From this perch, you are a raptor, and the battlefield is spread beneath you like a map.

Additionally, you have advantage on initiative rolls made while you are at a higher elevation than all hostile creatures in the encounter. Spire-Kin was forged from the highest point in the world, and it grants its wielder the high ground in every sense.

## Awakened State

Whisper the true name — the name of the highest point you have ever stood upon — from an equal or greater height. As a bonus action, speak the name. It might be a mountain peak, a dragon's back, the roof of a cathedral, the observation deck of a flying airship — whatever it is, you must be at least as high as that place was when you speak the name. If you are, the blade catches lightning.

A bolt of blue-white electricity arcs down from the sky — even if you are indoors or underground, it finds its way through stone and ceiling and earth, because this lightning is not weather but the blade's memory of the seven strikes that forged it. The rapier's needle-thin blade doubles in apparent length, now extending to a **reach of 10 feet**, and arcs of electricity play continuously along its edge. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the lightning bonus damage increases to **2d6**. Your Vertical Step improves — you can use it at will (still as a bonus action), the range increases to **90 feet**, and the force platform it creates persists for **1 minute** and can support up to two Medium or smaller creatures. You can create a number of platforms equal to your proficiency bonus at a time. Platforms beyond this limit wink out, oldest first.

When you hit a creature while awakened, you can choose to channel the blade's upward momentum into the strike — the target is launched **10 feet upward**, then lands prone. A creature that has no vertical space to rise into (such as in a low-ceilinged room) takes an additional **1d6 bludgeoning damage** instead as it slams into the ceiling.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can point Spire-Kin at a point you can see within 120 feet and call down **Sky-Sunder** — a bolt of lightning strikes that point from above in a **10-foot-radius, 60-foot-tall cylinder**. Each creature in the cylinder must make a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **10d6 lightning damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also launched 15 feet upward and lands prone. The point of impact erupts into a permanent **spire of solidified force energy** — a jagged pillar of translucent blue force 5 feet wide and 30 feet tall that provides full cover to creatures behind it. The spire persists for 10 minutes or until you use a bonus action to dismiss it.

## The True Name

The true name of Spire-Kin is the name of the highest point the wielder has ever stood upon — not a mountain range or a city district, but a specific peak, a specific rooftop, a specific place where you stood and looked down at the world and felt, for one moment, like you could touch the stars. The name must be whispered from an equal or greater height. The blade will not accept a name spoken from a valley — to know the spire, you must be worthy of the spire. The blade will signal its readiness by going perfectly still in your hand, its hum dropping to silence, waiting for you to realize where you need to be and how high you need to climb to get there.
