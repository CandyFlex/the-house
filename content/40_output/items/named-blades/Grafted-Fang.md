# Grafted Fang
*Weapon (longsword), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Grafted Fang was not forged. It was *grown* — gestated in the corpse-field of a dead star where the boundary between metal and flesh dissolved eons ago and never reformed. The blade is pale bone-white, veined with dark organic filaments that pulse slowly in a rhythm that does not match any living heartbeat. Where the blade meets the hilt, set into the metal like a cabochon in a ring, there is a suggestion of a closed eye — lidless, waiting. The grip wraps around the wielder's hand with a warmth that is neither comforting nor threatening. It is simply alive, and you are holding it, and it knows.

The blade is a symbiote — a creature of living metal and alien biology that bonds to the wielder's nervous system at the muscular level. It cannot be dropped or disarmed because it does not wish to leave. It drinks. It shares. It is, in the most literal sense, hungry.

The true name of Grafted Fang is not a sound. It is a sequence of muscle contractions — a specific pattern of tension and release along the forearm, bicep, and shoulder that the blade can feel through the nervous bond. To learn the name, the wielder must endure withdrawal: the blade must be starved — denied blood for days — until the wielder's body, in its desperation, learns the muscle-sequences that make the eye open. It is learned through pain and craving. Once learned, it cannot be forgotten — the body remembers what the mind cannot.

The spirit within Grafted Fang calls itself **The Needle**. It is hungry, but not malevolent — it is simply *alive*, and life consumes. It communicates through sensation: a tightening of the grip when danger approaches, a warm pulse when it is pleased, a cold dull ache when it has been denied for too long. The Needle does not see itself as separate from the wielder. It sees itself as an organ the wielder was born missing, finally returned.

When the name is invoked, the blade does not ignite or vanish. It *unfolds* — the grip splits and rotates, the blade splits laterally, forming an X-shaped double-blade that hums with a low, wet vibration. Tendrils of metallic flesh — silver-veined and glistening — erupt from the hilt and burrow into the wielder's forearm and upper arm, visibly pulsing as they drink and feed. The eye opens. It is the color of coagulated starlight.

## Properties

### Sealed State
*The Needle waits. It is patient, but not infinitely so.*

**+2 Weapon.** You have a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with Grafted Fang.

**Hungering Edge.** When you hit a creature with this weapon, the attack deals an additional 1d4 necrotic damage. You gain temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt. These temporary hit points stack with themselves, up to a maximum of your level + your proficiency bonus, and last until you finish a short or long rest.

**Kill-Feeder.** When you reduce a hostile creature to 0 hit points with Grafted Fang, the blade pulses with warmth and you gain advantage on the next attack roll you make before the end of your next turn. The Needle is *pleased*.

**Symbiotic Bond.** While Grafted Fang is drawn, it bonds to your hand at a muscular level. You cannot be disarmed of it. You can retract the bond as a bonus action, allowing you to sheathe or set down the blade. You are always considered proficient with Grafted Fang — the blade knows how to move, and your body follows. Additionally, you have advantage on saving throws against poison and disease; the Needle purifies what enters your bloodstream through its bond.

**Parasitic Transfusion.** When you receive magical healing from any source while holding Grafted Fang, you can choose to transfer any amount of that healing to a willing creature you can see within 30 feet of you. If you do, you gain an equal amount of temporary hit points (in addition to any from Hungering Edge, up to the usual cap). The blade converts your blood to theirs and rewards the sacrifice.

---

### Satiety
*The blade hungers. What it does not take from enemies, it takes from you.*

Grafted Fang has a Satiety score, representing how recently it has fed. Satiety functions as follows:

- When you hit a creature with Grafted Fang, the blade's Satiety increases by 1. Satiety cannot exceed 5.
- At the start of each of your turns, Satiety decreases by 1.
- If Satiety ever reaches 0, the blade feeds on you instead: at the start of each of your turns while Satiety is 0, you take 1d10 necrotic damage. This damage cannot be reduced or prevented. The blade is not punishing you — it is simply hungry, and you are the nearest source of life. The damage stops the moment Satiety rises above 0.
- Satiety resets to 0 when you roll initiative and at the end of combat.
- While Satiety is 4 or higher, the blade's eye — still closed while sealed — twitches faintly. It is anticipating.

### Awakened State — *"Grafted Fang"*
*Speak the name through your body. The eye opens. The Needle unfolds.*

**Invocation.** You can invoke the blade's true name as a bonus action — the sequence of muscle contractions your body learned through starvation. Alternatively, the first time you drop below half your hit point maximum after finishing a long rest, the blade awakens *involuntarily* (this counts as your one use of the invocation — the Needle decides you need it). The invocation lasts for 1 minute. Once you invoke the blade, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest. You can suppress an involuntary awakening as a reaction if you wish — the blade respects your choice, though the eye flickers with disappointment.

**Unfolded Form.** The blade splits into its X-shaped double-blade configuration. Tendrils of metallic flesh burrow into your forearm and upper arm, visibly pulsing. The eye at the hilt opens fully — it is the color of coagulated starlight, and it looks where you look. The blade can be wielded one-handed (versatile, 2d6 slashing + 2d6 necrotic on hit) or two-handed (2d8 slashing + 2d8 necrotic on hit). The X-shaped blade configuration grants the following passive benefit:

**X-Guard.** When a creature within 5 feet of you makes a melee attack against you while you are wielding Grafted Fang in its awakened form, you can use your reaction to add your proficiency bonus to your AC against that attack. If the attack misses, the attacker takes slashing damage equal to 2d6 + your proficiency bonus. This is a reflexive action — the blade's crossed form is a living parrying weapon.

**Awakened Satiety.** The Satiety system functions normally during the awakening (max 5, same spend costs, same 1d10/round starvation). However, while awakened, you can exceed the normal Satiety cap: Satiety can reach a temporary maximum of 7, and any points above 5 are lost when the awakening ends. Additionally, while awakened and Satiety is 5 or higher, your movement speed increases by 10 feet — the Needle is well-fed and eager.

**Polearm Form (Awakened).** While your blade is awakened, you can spend 2 Satiety as a bonus action to extend Grafted Fang into its polearm configuration for the remainder of the awakening (no duration limit). While extended, the blade has reach 10 feet, its damage dice become 1d10 slashing + 2d8 necrotic (one-handed) or 2d8 slashing + 2d8 necrotic (two-handed), and it gains the heavy property. You can retract it to X-blade form as a bonus action. You cannot use the X-Guard reaction while the blade is in polearm form — the crossed blades are needed for the reflexive parry.

**Chrysalis Bloom (1/Awakening).** As an action, you can release a 30-foot-radius burst of Needle-spores from the blade's open eye. Each creature of your choice in the area must make a DC 17 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, a creature takes 8d8 necrotic damage and becomes infested for 1 minute. While infested, a creature takes 2d8 necrotic damage at the start of each of its turns, and you gain temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt to infested creatures by this effect. On a success, a creature takes half damage and is not infested. Using Chrysalis Bloom immediately ends the awakening — the Needle has spent everything it had. The blade returns to its sealed state (the eye closes, slowly, reluctantly).

## Balance Notes
Grafted Fang is a resource-management weapon built around a push-your-luck mechanic. The Satiety system creates a combat rhythm: you must hit every round or the blade starts drinking from you. This punishes defensive play and rewards aggression — exactly the fantasy a parasitic symbiote blade should evoke.

At Very Rare, the sealed state damage (1d8+1d4+2+STR, ~14 one-handed) is modest — comparable to a *sword of wounding* (rare, 1d4 necrotic per hit stacking) with the same enhancement bonus but with THP generation and significant utility (disarm immunity, healing transfer, poison/disease saves). The Satiety spends in sealed form give tactical flexibility without the full power spike of the awakening.

The awakened state transforms the blade into a flexible combat tool. The X-Guard reaction provides consistent defense, the increased Satiety cap and speed bonus reward prolonged combat, and the polearm extension gives reach at the cost of Satiety and the loss of the X-Guard passive — a genuine tradeoff.

The Satiety starvation mechanic (1d10/round when dry) creates genuine tension. A bad round of misses can cascade into self-damage. This makes Grafted Fang a high-skill weapon — it rewards players who hit consistently and punishes those who don't. The involuntary first awakening (below half HP) is the needle's personality bleeding through the mechanics — it cares about you, in its way.

Chrysalis Bloom as an awakening-ender is intentionally dramatic. 8d8 + ongoing damage + THP drain could turn a fight, but it costs you the rest of your awakening. It's a "break glass in case of emergency" button, not a rotation ability.

The THP stacking from both Hungering Edge and Parasitic Transfusion creates a durable frontline fighter who sustains through aggression rather than healing — a distinct playstyle from traditional tanks.

## Variants
**Grafted Fang, Chrysalis Eternal (Legendary).** +3 longsword. Satiety cap increases to 8 (10 awakened). Starvation damage becomes 1d6 (instead of 1d10). While Satiety is 5 or higher, you have a flying speed of 30 feet (tendrils form temporary wings of metallic flesh). Chrysalis Bloom does not end the awakening. Polearm form no longer costs Satiety to extend during awakening. Cost: 14,000,000 soul stones.
