# Grafted Fang
*Weapon (longsword), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Grafted Fang was not forged. It was *grown* — gestated in the corpse-field of a dead star where the boundary between metal and flesh dissolved eons ago and never reformed. The blade is pale bone-white, veined with dark organic filaments that pulse slowly in a rhythm that does not match any living heartbeat. Where the blade meets the hilt, set into the metal like a cabochon in a ring, there is a suggestion of a closed eye — lidless, waiting. The grip wraps around the wielder's hand with a warmth that is neither comforting nor threatening. It is simply alive, and you are holding it, and it knows.

The blade is a symbiote — a creature of living metal and alien biology that bonds to the wielder's nervous system at the muscular level. It cannot be dropped or disarmed because it does not wish to leave. It drinks. It shares. It is, in the most literal sense, hungry.

The true name of Grafted Fang is not a sound. It is a sequence of muscle contractions — a specific pattern of tension and release along the forearm, bicep, and shoulder that the blade can feel through the nervous bond. To learn the name, the wielder must endure withdrawal: the blade must be starved — denied blood for days — until the wielder's body, in its desperation, learns the muscle-sequences that make the eye open. It is learned through pain and craving. Once learned, it cannot be forgotten — the body remembers what the mind cannot.

The spirit within Grafted Fang calls itself **The Needle**. It is hungry, but not malevolent — it is simply *alive*, and life consumes. It communicates through sensation: a tightening of the grip when danger approaches, a warm pulse when it is pleased, a cold dull ache when it has been denied for too long. The Needle does not see itself as separate from the wielder. It sees itself as an organ the wielder was born missing, finally returned.

When the name is invoked, the blade does not ignite or vanish. It *unfolds* — the grip splits and rotates, the blade splits laterally, forming an X-shaped double-blade that hums with a low, wet vibration. Tendrils of metallic flesh — silver-veined and glistening — erupt from the hilt and burrow into the wielder's forearm and upper arm, visibly pulsing as they drink and feed. The eye opens. It is the color of coagulated starlight.

## Properties

Grafted Fang is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. The Needle is hungry. When you hit a creature with this weapon, the attack deals an additional **1d4 necrotic damage**, and you gain temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt. These temporary hit points stack with themselves, up to a maximum of your level plus your proficiency bonus, and last until you finish a short or long rest.

When you reduce a hostile creature to 0 hit points with Grafted Fang, the blade pulses with warmth and you gain advantage on the next attack roll you make before the end of your next turn. The Needle is *pleased*.

While drawn, Grafted Fang bonds to your hand at a muscular level. You cannot be disarmed of it. You can retract the bond as a bonus action, allowing you to sheathe or set down the blade. You are always considered proficient with Grafted Fang — the blade knows how to move, and your body follows. Additionally, you have advantage on saving throws against poison and disease; the Needle purifies what enters your bloodstream through its bond.

When you receive magical healing from any source while holding Grafted Fang, you can choose to transfer any amount of that healing to a willing creature you can see within **30 feet** of you. If you do, you gain an equal amount of temporary hit points — in addition to any from Hungering Edge, up to the usual cap. The blade converts your blood to theirs and rewards the sacrifice.

---

Grafted Fang feeds, and its hunger is measured. The blade has a **Satiety score** representing how recently it has fed. When you hit a creature with Grafted Fang, Satiety increases by 1. Satiety cannot exceed **5**. At the start of each of your turns, Satiety decreases by 1. If Satiety ever reaches 0, the blade feeds on you instead: at the start of each of your turns while Satiety is 0, you take **1d10 necrotic damage**. This damage cannot be reduced or prevented. The blade is not punishing you — it is simply hungry, and you are the nearest source of life. The damage stops the moment Satiety rises above 0. Satiety resets to 0 when you roll initiative and at the end of combat. While Satiety is 4 or higher, the blade's eye — still closed — twitches faintly. It is anticipating.

You can spend Satiety points at any time, no action required, to fuel the blade's hungrier abilities. Spending **1 point of Satiety**, you may activate **Deep Drink** — the next time you hit a creature this turn, the attack deals an additional **2d6 necrotic damage**. Alternatively, you may activate **Lashing Reach**, extending the next melee attack you make this turn by **5 feet** as tendrils briefly extend the blade. Or, as a reaction when a creature hits you with a melee attack, you may activate **X-Guard Riposte**, adding your proficiency bonus to your AC against that attack; if the attack misses, the attacker takes **2d6 slashing damage** as the blade's instinctive cross-guard reflex lashes out.

Spending **2 points of Satiety**, you may make one additional melee weapon attack with Grafted Fang as a bonus action on this turn in a **Frenzied Strike**. Or, you may extend Grafted Fang into a **Polearm Extension** — until the end of your next turn, the blade's damage die becomes **1d10 slashing**, its necrotic bonus becomes **1d8** instead of 1d4, its reach becomes **10 feet**, and it gains the heavy property. You can revert to normal form as a bonus action.

Spending **3 points of Satiety**, you may unleash **Sanguine Reach**, which recharges on a roll of 5–6 at the start of your turn. Tendrils lash from your arm in a **15-foot cone**. Each creature in the cone must make a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **6d6 necrotic damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. You regain hit points equal to half the total necrotic damage dealt.

---

Speak the name through your body. The eye opens. The Needle unfolds.

You can invoke the blade's true name as a bonus action — the sequence of muscle contractions your body learned through starvation. Alternatively, the first time you drop below half your hit point maximum after finishing a long rest, the blade awakens involuntarily — this counts as your one use of the invocation. The Needle decides you need it. The awakening lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest. You can suppress an involuntary awakening as a reaction if you wish; the blade respects your choice, though the eye flickers with disappointment.

When awakened, the blade splits into its X-shaped double-blade configuration. Tendrils of metallic flesh burrow into your forearm and upper arm, visibly pulsing. The eye at the hilt opens fully — it is the color of coagulated starlight, and it looks where you look. The blade can be wielded one-handed, dealing **2d6 slashing plus 2d6 necrotic** damage on a hit, or two-handed, dealing **2d8 slashing plus 2d8 necrotic**. The crossed blades form a living parrying weapon. When a creature within **5 feet** of you makes a melee attack against you, you can use your reaction to add your proficiency bonus to your AC against that attack in a reflexive **X-Guard**. If the attack misses, the attacker takes slashing damage equal to **2d6 plus your proficiency bonus**.

The Satiety system functions normally during the awakening, but with a vicious edge: the Satiety cap temporarily rises to **7** (points above 5 are lost when the awakening ends). While Satiety is 5 or higher, your movement speed increases by **10 feet** — the Needle is well-fed and eager.

While awakened, you can spend 2 Satiety as a bonus action to extend Grafted Fang into its polearm configuration for the remainder of the awakening. While extended, the blade has reach **10 feet**, its damage dice become **1d10 slashing plus 2d8 necrotic** (one-handed) or **2d8 slashing plus 2d8 necrotic** (two-handed), and it gains the heavy property. You can retract it to X-blade form as a bonus action. You cannot use the X-Guard reaction while the blade is in polearm form — the crossed blades are needed for the reflexive parry.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can release a **30-foot-radius** burst of Needle-spores from the blade's open eye — a **Chrysalis Bloom**. Each creature of your choice in the area must make a **DC 17 Constitution saving throw**. On a failure, a creature takes **8d8 necrotic damage** and becomes infested for 1 minute, taking **2d8 necrotic damage** at the start of each of its turns; you gain temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt to infested creatures by this effect. On a success, a creature takes half damage and is not infested. Using Chrysalis Bloom immediately ends the awakening — the Needle has spent everything it had. The eye closes, slowly, reluctantly.
