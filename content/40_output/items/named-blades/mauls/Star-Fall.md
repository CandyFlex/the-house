# Star-Fall
*Weapon (maul), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Star-Fall was forged from the core of a meteorite that spent a billion years falling. Not falling toward anything — just falling, through interstellar void, past dead stars and unborn galaxies, accumulating speed and mass and a kind of cosmic loneliness that words cannot describe. It entered the atmosphere of the world where it would eventually be forged in the same year that the first mortal civilization learned to work metal, and it took another three thousand years to reach the surface — slowed by sheer stubbornness, by the meteorite's refusal to burn up before its purpose was fulfilled. When it finally struck, it did not crater. It landed — gently, almost politely — in the center of a smith's forge, already at the perfect temperature for working. The smith, who had been expecting it (she had dreamed of the meteorite every night for forty years), shaped its core into a maul without ever applying heat. The metal was already hot — still carrying the friction of a billion-year descent.

The maul's head pulses with a deep redshift, like a dying sun compressed into a hammer. The color shifts subtly as you watch it — now crimson, now orange, now a deep, sullen red that seems to have weight. The haft is dark iron wrapped with bands of nickel from the meteorite's mantle, and the grip is warm — always warm, as if the friction of the fall has not yet dissipated and never will. When Star-Fall is swung, the head leaves faint red after-images in the air, momentarily painting the path of every arc like a meteor shower frozen in time.

The consciousness within Star-Fall is patient in a way that makes Sundial look impatient. It fell for a billion years. It can wait. It communicates through gravity — a subtle pull toward threats, a slight heaviness when you are heading the wrong direction, a weightless lift when you are on the right path. It favors wielders who understand that some things take time, who can commit to a course of action and see it through to the end, who would fall for a billion years to hit the right target. It dislikes hesitation, second-guessing, and people who change their minds mid-swing.

## Sealed State

Star-Fall is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, in addition to the weapon's normal **2d6 bludgeoning damage**, the maul deals an additional **2d6 force damage** — the kinetic residue of a billion years of uninterrupted falling, released on impact.

When you hit a creature with Star-Fall, you can choose to embed a **fragment** of the maul's meteorite metal in the target's flesh — a sliver of cosmic iron no larger than a fingernail, glowing faintly with residual redshift. At the start of the creature's next turn, the fragment erupts in a **Sub-Detonation**: the creature and each other creature within **10 feet** of it must make a **DC 20 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **4d6 force damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. Once you embed a fragment, you cannot embed another until the start of your next turn — the maul needs a moment to grow another sliver.

While on the same plane as Star-Fall, you always know the exact direction and distance to the maul. If you hurl it into the sea or lose it in a chasm, you can find it again — it pulls at you, subtly, the way a distant mass pulls at an orbiting body. Additionally, while under an open sky at night — truly under the stars, not merely outdoors — you have advantage on attack rolls with Star-Fall against creatures not native to the current plane. The stars see what does not belong, and they want it removed.

## Awakened State

The true name of Star-Fall is a sequence of radio frequencies — the sound the meteorite made as it entered the atmosphere, a descending whistle that stretched across the entire electromagnetic spectrum. To learn it, you must hold the maul's head against your ear in absolute stillness — no movement, no speech, no heartbeat faster than resting — and listen. The blade replays its entry in miniature: a sound that begins beyond hearing and descends through static and whistle into a low, vibrating hum. The sequence is the name. It cannot be written down. It can only be remembered.

Speak the name as an action — not with your voice, but by humming the frequency sequence in the exact descending order — and the maul remembers what it was to fall. The head splits open along invisible seams, the metal plates separating like a blooming flower to reveal the **Star-Heart**: a miniature sun, compressed to the size of a fist, burning in the maul's core with light that is visible through closed eyelids. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the force bonus damage increases to **3d6**, for a total of 2d6 bludgeoning + 3d6 force on each hit. The fragment embed becomes more volatile — the Sub-Detonation deals **6d6 force damage** in a **15-foot radius**. Additionally, the fragment now detonates immediately if the embedded creature is reduced to 0 hit points before its turn, and the detonation radius doubles to 30 feet in such cases.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, the Star-Heart pulses — each creature of your choice within **10 feet** of you takes **1d6 fire damage**. The heat is not extreme, but it is constant, the radiance of a star that has been falling for so long it has forgotten how to be cold.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can hurl Star-Fall at a point you can see within **120 feet** and call down **Meteor Strike**. The maul leaves your hand and arcs through the air like a shooting star, trailing red-and-gold light. It lands at the target point and detonates in a **30-foot radius**. Each creature in the area must make a **DC 20 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **10d6 force damage plus 10d6 fire damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. The impact site becomes a **Meteor Scar** — the area is difficult terrain, and any creature that ends its turn in the scar takes **2d6 fire damage**. The scar persists for 1 minute. At the end of your next turn, the maul reconstitutes itself in your hand, the Star-Heart still pulsing, as if it had never left. You cannot use Meteor Strike again during the same awakening.

## The True Name

The true name of Star-Fall is a descending sequence of radio frequencies — the sound of the meteorite entering atmosphere a billion years after it began to fall. The blade replays this sound constantly, but at a volume so low and a frequency so high that it cannot be heard by any natural means. The wielder must hold the maul's head directly against their ear while remaining absolutely still — no movement, no speech, a heartbeat slowed to resting rhythm — and listen for at least 1 minute. During this time, the wielder must maintain concentration as if on a spell (DC 10 Constitution saving throw each round that external distractions occur). The frequency sequence will descend into audible range, and the wielder must memorize it — a descending set of notes like a whistle falling from the sky. To speak the name, the wielder must hum the sequence in perfect order, and the blade will judge whether the memory is true.
