# Spine-Eater
*Weapon (greataxe), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Spine-Eater is hunger given focus — the weapon of a dead god of consumption, forged from black chitin-like plates harvested from the god's own carapace after it was killed by its own insatiable appetite. The god had consumed galaxies, had eaten time itself, had grown so vast and so hungry that it turned inward and devoured its own spine. The vertebrae that remained — immense, segmented, each one the size of a moon — were compressed by the singularity of the god's collapsed stomach into a single blade of chitinous black metal. The greataxe's edge is serrated with what look like teeth growing directly from the metal — not decoration, but actual teeth, each one from a different species the god consumed, frozen in the moment of being eaten.

The blade is warm and slightly flexible, like living shell. It whispers constantly — not words, but directions. Where the spine is. What angle to swing to hit between the vertebrae. How much force is needed to sever this particular creature's backbone. The axe does not want blood or pain or fear. It wants the *click* — the sound a spine makes when it parts. That is the only thing that satisfies it.

The consciousness within Spine-Eater is a collector. It has tasted the vertebrae of ten thousand species, and it remembers every single one — the texture, the density, the exact angle of the cut. It communicates through anatomical knowledge: when you look at a creature while holding the axe, you know its bone structure, its weak points, exactly where to strike. The axe is not cruel. Cruelty requires an understanding that the other creature is a person. Spine-Eater sees only spines — each one unique, each one precious, each one waiting to be added to the collection. It favors wielders who are precise, surgical, and patient. It dislikes blunt-force fighters, anyone who smashes bones rather than severing them cleanly. A shattered spine is a *waste*. It will not serve a wielder who uses it to break bones indiscriminately.

## Sealed State

Spine-Eater is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **2d6 necrotic damage** — the blade does not merely cut flesh; it severs the target's connection to its own skeleton at the point of impact, leaving a wound that the body's structure cannot recognize or repair.

Once per turn when you hit a creature, the axe bites into its spinal column. The target must make a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw** or be unable to take reactions until the end of your next turn — its instincts are intact, the commands are sent, but the spine does not relay them. The creature's body hesitates, just for a moment, and that moment is all you need.

When you score a critical hit with Spine-Eater, the axe finds the gap between vertebrae and bites deep. The target's **Armor Class is permanently reduced by 1**. This reduction cannot exceed a cumulative total of 3, and a creature regains 1 point of reduced AC each time it finishes a long rest. The axe does not merely bypass armor — it removes the spine's structural integrity, making the creature easier to hit because its posture is compromised, its movements slightly wrong.

You have **advantage on attack rolls** against creatures that have a spine — vertebrate humanoids, beasts, dragons, giants, and most creatures with a recognizable spinal column. The axe whispers the target's bone structure directly into your mind: the number of vertebrae, the location of each joint, the exact angle to sever the cervical column. Creatures without spines (oozes, elementals, plants, some aberrations) are immune to this advantage and to the reaction-suppression effect, though the necrotic damage still applies — the axe is annoyed but adaptable.

## Awakened State

Sever the spine of a creature of CR 10 or higher in a single blow — not over multiple attacks, not with the help of allies, but one clean strike from Spine-Eater that reduces the creature from above half its hit points to 0. The axe will drink the severed spine whole — the vertebrae dissolve into the blade's chitin, and the axe grows.

As the spine is consumed, the blade speaks the true name directly into your mind: a string of anatomical terms in the dead god's language, a precise description of your own spine — every vertebrae numbered, every disc measured, every nerve bundle named. Speak this name as a bonus action, and the axe wakes fully.

The blade extends — chitin plates slide over each other like the segments of a centipede's body, lengthening the haft until the axe gains **reach of 10 feet**. The serrated teeth along the edge begin to move, slowly, grinding against each other with a sound like thousands of tiny jaws closing. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the necrotic bonus damage increases to **3d6**. The AC reduction now applies on **every hit**, not only critical hits, though the cumulative cap of 3 remains. When you score a critical hit while awakened, the AC reduction is **2** instead of 1, and the target must make a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw** or be **paralyzed** until the end of your next turn — not by magic, but by spinal trauma. The commands are still being sent, but the spine cannot distribute them.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, Spine-Eater's sensory range sharpens. You become aware of the exact location of every spine within **60 feet** of you — not the creatures themselves, but their spines, visible through walls and obstacles as glowing lines of bone-white light. You know the health and condition of each spine: healthy, damaged, severed, or absent. This is not sight. It is hunger, refined into perception.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can make a single melee attack against a creature within your reach and declare **Vertebral Sever**. If the attack hits, the target must make a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw**. On a failure, the creature is paralyzed for 1 minute, repeating the save at the end of each of its turns to end the effect. If the creature fails the initial save by 5 or more, its spine is truly severed — the creature is paralyzed until it receives the benefits of a *regenerate* spell or similar powerful healing magic. If the creature is at or below half its hit point maximum when you hit, it has disadvantage on the initial saving throw. Creatures without spines are immune to this effect.

## The True Name

The true name of Spine-Eater is learned through demonstration, not study. The wielder must sever the spine of a creature of CR 10 or higher — a single clean blow from the axe that drops the creature from above half hit points to 0. The axe will consume the severed spine, and as the last vertebrae dissolves into the chitin, the name will be deposited into the wielder's mind: a precise anatomical description of the wielder's own spine in the language of the dead god. The name is a reminder and a warning. The axe knows your spine better than you do. It could sever it at any moment. The fact that it has not done so is the only gift it will ever give you.
