# Brine-Singer
*Weapon (trident), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Brine-Singer was forged in the crushing darkness of the deepest sea — not by a smith, but by the pressure itself, layering black coral and abyssal mineral over the vertebrae of a dead leviathan over the course of ten thousand years. No fire touched it. No hammer struck it. The forge was the weight of the ocean, and the anvil was a trench so deep that light had never reached its floor. The resulting trident is black coral shot through with veins of phosphorescent blue — three tines that curve slightly inward like the arms of an anemone, weeping cold seawater in a constant, silent trickle.

The blade is never dry. Place it in a desert, and water will bead on its surface. Place it in a vault, and the vault will smell of salt and depth within a day. The water it weeps is not ordinary seawater — it is the water of the abyss, cold enough to numb fingers and thick with the memory of pressure that could crush steel. Creatures of the deep — fish, cephalopods, things without names — are drawn to it, surfacing where they should not be, watching from the shallows with eyes adapted to permanent darkness.

The consciousness within Brine-Singer is a singer. It does not speak — it sings, in frequencies that travel better through water than air, whale-song and current-hum and the creak of ice shelves calving into polar seas. It communicates through melody: a rising note means danger approaching, a falling note means safety, a complex harmony means the blade is thinking about something deeply. It favors wielders who listen more than they speak — sailors, fishermen, anyone who has ever sat alone on a shore and watched the tide go out. It dislikes fire, dryness, and people who cannot swim. If you try to light a torch near Brine-Singer, the blade will weep harder, dousing the flame with a splash of seawater that comes from nowhere and reeks of the deep.

## Sealed State

Brine-Singer is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **1d6 cold damage** — the cold of the lightless deep, where the water is only a few degrees above freezing and nothing warm has ever lived.

When you hit a creature with Brine-Singer, you can choose to leave a **5-foot-radius puddle** of seawater in the creature's space. The puddle is difficult terrain for creatures without a swim speed, and it persists for 1 minute before soaking into the ground. You can maintain a number of puddles equal to your proficiency bonus at one time. The blade chooses where the water goes.

While holding Brine-Singer, you gain a **swim speed equal to your walking speed** and the ability to breathe underwater. You are immune to the effects of deep water pressure and suffer no penalties for operating at depth. The blade adjusts the water around you — you move through oceans as easily as through air, and the cold of the deep does not touch you.

Once per short rest, as an action, you can command the blade to sing. The **Siren's Pull** emanates from you in a 20-foot radius — each creature of your choice within range must succeed on a **DC 17 Wisdom saving throw** or use its reaction to move up to its speed directly toward you by the most direct route. The song is beautiful and terrible, a whale's love call twisted into a predator's lure. Creatures that cannot hear are immune to this effect.

## Awakened State

Hold Brine-Singer while fully submerged in the deepest natural body of water you can find — an ocean trench, a bottomless lake, a sea that predates continents. Descend until the light fails and the cold becomes absolute, and listen. The blade will sing a single word in whale-song — a subsonic vibration that travels through your bones rather than your ears. Speak that word as an action, and the blade becomes a wave.

The trident's form blurs — the three tines dissolve into liquid, then reform as a wave-shape of semi-solid water, extending and contracting with a rhythm that matches distant tides. Water swirls around the blade in a continuous vortex, and the weeping seawater becomes a torrent — trails of brine spiral from the tines like comet tails. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, your attacks with Brine-Singer gain **reach of 10 feet** as the water-form extends the blade's effective striking surface. The cold bonus damage increases to **2d6**. Additionally, the **puddle radius expands to 10 feet**, and any creature of your choice that ends its turn in a puddle takes **2d6 cold damage** as the deep-sea water saps its warmth.

The Siren's Pull gains potency while the blade is awakened — its radius extends to **30 feet**, and creatures that fail the save must also use their action on their next turn to move toward you (dashing if necessary). The song has become a command, not a suggestion.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, you may choose one puddle within 60 feet. The puddle erupts upward in a **Geyser** — a column of freezing seawater 10 feet wide and 20 feet high. Each creature in the column must make a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **3d6 cold damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also pushed 10 feet upward and lands prone. The geyser lingers as a permanent 5-foot-wide pillar of churning water until the awakening ends, providing half cover to creatures behind it and extinguishing nonmagical fires within 5 feet.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can release the full voice of the deep — an **Abyssal Chorus** in a **30-foot cone** originating from you. Each creature in the cone must make a **DC 17 Constitution saving throw**, taking **8d6 thunder damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also **stunned** until the end of your next turn as the whale-song frequency scrambles its nervous system. The chorus is audible for up to 1 mile underwater and 500 feet through air.

## The True Name

The true name of Brine-Singer is a single note — a subsonic frequency that the blade produces only when submerged in deep water, so low that it is felt rather than heard. The wielder must take the blade into the deepest natural body of water they can reach — not a well, not a pond, not a harbor, but true depth: an ocean trench, an abyssal lake, a sea cave that descends into darkness. At maximum depth, the wielder must press their ear against the trident's shaft and listen. The blade will sing one word — but in whale-song, a language of pressure and vibration that cannot be heard in air. The wielder must learn to reproduce that vibration in their own throat, a sound that should be impossible for humanoid vocal cords. It is not. The body remembers the ocean, if you go deep enough to remind it.
