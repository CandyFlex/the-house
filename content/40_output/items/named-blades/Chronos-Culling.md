# Chronos Culling
*Weapon (longsword), legendary (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Chronos Culling was never forged in the conventional sense. It was *severed* — cut away from the timestream at a point where two incompatible futures intersected and one had to be sacrificed. The paradox of that moment — a timeline that both happened and didn't — was extracted by entities unknown, compressed, and shaped into a blade that exists slightly ahead of its own present.

The blade is silver-white with a permanent blur at its edge — not motion blur, but *temporal* blur, the blade existing across multiple adjacent moments simultaneously, each one fractionally offset from the last. Looking at it directly causes a faint pressure behind the eyes and a sense of déjà vu that deepens the longer you stare. Sometimes, in peripheral vision, you catch the blade in a position it wasn't in a moment ago. Sometimes you catch it *before* it moves. These are not illusions. The blade exists in a superposition of moments, and your eyes are simply not built to resolve them all at once.

The true name of Chronos Culling is not a sound, not a word, not a frequency, not a shape of silence — it is a *moment*. A single instant of absolute consequence that the wielder must witness while holding the blade: a death, a birth, a betrayal, a forgiveness, the exact second a decision branches reality. The blade will show you the moment when it comes. Your task is to recognize it. Many wielders have held Chronos Culling for years and never learned its name, because they were looking for a word when they should have been paying attention to their own lives. The blade does not reward cleverness. It rewards presence.

When the name is invoked — the moment recognized and accepted — the blade does not erupt or vanish or unfold. It *splits*. Three translucent temporal echoes fan out from the hilt in a ghostly arc: the blade as it was one second ago, the blade as it is now, the blade as it will be one second from now. All three are real. All three can cut. And the wielder's personal timestream accelerates — moving faster than causality, acting when others are still reacting, striking from moments that haven't happened yet.

The spirit within Chronos Culling is neither proud nor hungry nor patient. It is *urgent*. It communicates through the blade's blur — a flicker that means "move," a shimmer that means "wait," a sudden clarity that means "now." It has seen every possible version of the next five seconds and it is trying, desperately, to tell you which one you should choose. It does not speak because speech takes time, and time is the one thing it cannot spare.

## Properties

Chronos Culling is a **+3 weapon**, granting a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **2d6 force damage** — the blade's edge exists slightly out of phase with normal matter. It does not cut flesh so much as it severs the target's connection to the present moment.

Every strike drags the target out of temporal sync. When you hit a creature with Chronos Culling, the target's speed is reduced by **10 feet** until the end of your next turn, as its personal timestream decouples slightly from objective time. This effect stacks. If a creature's speed is reduced to 0 by this effect, it must succeed on a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw** at the end of its turn or be paralyzed until the end of your next turn — trapped in the wrong second, unable to act in the one where everything else is happening. Creatures immune to the paralyzed condition are not immune; they are not paralyzed, they are *absent*.

Initiative rolls made while holding Chronos Culling have advantage. Once per round, when a creature you can see misses you with an attack roll, you can use your reaction to make one melee weapon attack against that creature — you saw the miss coming half a second before the attacker decided to swing.

You are **immune to the slowed condition**, and your speed cannot be reduced by magical effects, though difficult terrain and other physical impediments still apply. Effects that would banish you to another plane, shunt you forward in time, or otherwise remove you from the present moment — *banishment*, *maze*, a sphinx's lair action — are made at disadvantage against you, and you have advantage on saving throws against them. You are too firmly rooted in your own timestream to be easily displaced.

Recognize the moment. The blade splits. You exist in multiple seconds at once.

As a bonus action, you recognize the name — the moment the blade showed you, finally understood. The blade splits into three translucent temporal echoes that fan out from the hilt in a ghostly arc: the blade-as-it-was, the blade-as-it-is, and the blade-as-it-will-be. All three are real. Your personal timestream accelerates. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, Chronos Culling can be wielded one-handed or two-handed, gaining the versatile property. Damage becomes **1d8 slashing plus 3d6 force** (one-handed) or **2d8 slashing plus 3d6 force** (two-handed). The Temporal Lag speed reduction and paralysis effects from the sealed state continue to apply when you hit.

On each of your turns while the blade is awakened, you can take one **additional action**. This can be used to take the Attack action (one weapon attack only, not the full Attack action), Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, or Use an Object. You cannot use it to cast a spell or activate a magic item. You are not faster — you are *earlier*, acting in the gaps between moments where no one else is moving yet.

At the start of each of your turns, a translucent echo of yourself — a version of you from approximately half a second in the future — flickers into existence in an unoccupied space of your choice within **5 feet** of you. This **Temporal Echo** can make one melee weapon attack against a creature within its reach, using your attack bonus and dealing force damage instead of the weapon's normal damage type, since the echo's blade has no physical edge, only temporal. The echo scores a critical hit on a roll of **19 or 20**. After making its attack, the echo vanishes until the start of your next turn. The echo is not a creature; it cannot be targeted, damaged, or affected by any effect.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can sever a creature's connection to the present moment entirely. Choose a creature you can see within **60 feet** of you. It must make a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, the creature vanishes — shunted exactly 1 round into the future. At the start of your next turn, it reappears in the space it vanished from, or the nearest unoccupied space if that space is now occupied, with the same hit points, conditions, and ongoing effects it had when it vanished. The creature has no memory of the intervening time — to it, the world simply skipped. Creatures that cannot be affected by time-altering magic, at the DM's discretion (such as creatures specifically noted as existing outside of time), are immune. On a successful save, the creature takes **8d10 psychic damage** and has its speed halved until the end of your next turn as its mind recoils from glimpsing its own severed timeline branching into paths that should not exist.
