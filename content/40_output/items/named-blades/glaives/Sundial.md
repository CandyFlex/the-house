# Sundial
*Weapon (glaive), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Sundial was forged at the exact moment of the autumnal equinox on a plane where time flows in spirals rather than lines. The smith was a chronomancer who had grown tired of immortality and wished to make something that would outlast even time itself — a weapon that would stand at the center of the temporal spiral and mark every passing moment as a blow against eternity. The glaive's blade is a flat disc of bronze three feet across, inscribed with celestial markings in a language that predates spoken words — concentric circles, radial lines, constellations that no longer exist. The disc casts no shadow and reflects no light. Instead, it shows the position of the sun at this exact latitude on this exact date, regardless of the actual weather or time of day. At noon, the disc blazes white-gold. At midnight, it goes dark as void.

The consciousness within Sundial is patient to the point of passivity. It has measured the passage of every moment since the day it was forged, and it will measure every moment until the last star goes dark. It communicates through timing — the disc will glint in your peripheral vision exactly when you should move, pulse warmth when you should wait, flash cold when you are about to make a mistake you cannot undo. It does not give advice or warnings in words. It simply tells you *when*. The blade favors wielders who understand that timing is everything — patient tacticians, duelists who wait for the perfect opening, anyone who has ever said "not yet." It dislikes the impulsive, the reckless, and people who are always early to appointments. To Sundial, rushing is a form of lying to time.

## Sealed State

Sundial is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. It has the reach property. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **1d6 force damage** — the disc's edge cuts not through flesh but through the target's position in time, leaving a wound that exists slightly out of sync with the present moment.

Once per turn, when you hit a creature with Sundial, you steal a fragment of its temporal momentum — you gain **+5 feet of movement speed** until the end of your current turn. This bonus stacks if you hit multiple times across different turns, to a maximum of +20 feet. The disc spins slightly faster with each accumulated moment, a soft hum building in its rotation.

Once per short rest, as a bonus action, you can activate **Temporal Rewind**. You vanish from your current space and reappear in the space you occupied at the start of your current turn. Any conditions, damage, or other effects that were applied to you this turn remain — you are rewinding your position, not your state. But you are no longer where the enemy expected you to be, and that is often enough.

While holding Sundial, you always know the exact time, date, and astronomical position relative to your current location — down to the second. You cannot be surprised. You see the moment of ambush arriving half a heartbeat before it happens, and the disc pulses cold against your palm to warn you.

## Awakened State

Learn the exact moment of sunrise at the place where Sundial was forged — not an approximation, but the precise second the sun's disc first broke the horizon on the date of the forging — and speak that timestamp aloud. As a bonus action, you declare the name: a date, a time, and the geographic coordinates of a chronomancer's forge that exists only within a spiral of frozen time. The disc begins to spin — slowly at first, then faster, until it is a blur of bronze light. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the movement speed bonus per hit increases to **+10 feet**, stacking to a maximum of +30 feet. The force bonus damage increases to **2d6**. Additionally, on each of your turns, you gain one additional **bonus action** that can be used only to take the Dash or Disengage action. You are not merely faster — you are slipping between moments, finding extra motion in the spaces between seconds.

At the start of each of your turns while awakened, the disc's rotation reaches a harmonic frequency and you may choose one willing creature you can see within 30 feet to **Swap Moments** with — you and the target exchange positions in the initiative order. If this would move the target's turn to a point in the round that has already passed, the target instead takes its turn immediately after yours in the current round. A creature can only be affected by Swap Moments once per awakening.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can plant Sundial into the ground and declare **Noon Strike**. The disc stops spinning, locks into the high-noon position, and releases a line of blinding temporal energy — a stroke of noon made physical. The line is **60 feet long and 5 feet wide**. Each creature in the line must make a **DC 17 Dexterity saving throw**, taking **8d8 force damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also **restrained** until the end of your next turn as its personal timestream is frozen at exactly noon — unable to progress into the afternoon or retreat into the morning. A creature restrained this way repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.

## The True Name

The true name of Sundial is not a word — it is a timestamp. The exact moment of sunrise at the blade's forging place, measured to the second. To discover it, the wielder must study the blade's celestial markings — the concentric circles and constellation lines encode star charts that can be used to triangulate the location of the forge. Once the location is identified (a task requiring proficiency in navigator's tools or a substantial astronomical library), the wielder must then calculate the exact solar rise time for the date of the autumnal equinox at that location. This is the name: a date, a latitude, a longitude, and a time. Sundial will confirm the calculation by spinning to a stop exactly as the wielder speaks the final second.
