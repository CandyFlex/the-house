# Lament
*Weapon (warhammer), very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
Lament was forged in grief — not the grief of mortals, but the grief of a god who loved a mortal and watched them die of old age in what felt, to the god, like a single afternoon. The forge-fire was the god's own anguish given physical heat; the hammer blows were the god's fists against the anvil of a dying star; the quenching was in tears — real tears, divine tears, the only ones that god ever shed. The resulting warhammer is dark, pitted iron that always feels cold and slightly damp, as though it has just been pulled from water. Veins of lighter grey trace through the head like tear-tracks down a face. The grip is wrapped in something that might be leather and might be preserved skin — the god's own palmprint, burned into the handle, still warm.

The consciousness within Lament does not have a name. It is the god's grief, given awareness but no identity beyond what it lost. It communicates through weight — the hammer grows heavier when you are lying to yourself, lighter when you are facing a truth you've been avoiding. It does not want vengeance or justice or closure. It wants company. It wants someone else to understand what it feels like to miss something so badly that the missing becomes a physical presence — a weight you carry because setting it down would feel like betrayal. The hammer will not serve anyone who has never lost anything. To the griefless, it is impossibly heavy — 500 pounds of dead iron that will not lift from the ground. But to someone carrying genuine sorrow, it is as light as a held memory.

---

## Sealed State

Lament is a **+2 weapon**, granting a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When you hit a creature with it, the attack deals an additional **1d6 psychic damage** as the hammer's grief bleeds into the target's emotional core.

Each time you strike a creature, the hammer tolls with the resonance of loss. The target must make a **DC 17 Wisdom saving throw** or become **overwhelmed with sorrow** until the end of your next turn. While overwhelmed, the creature has disadvantage on attack rolls — its arms feel heavy, its will sluggish, every swing an effort against a crushing sadness that has no source but feels entirely real.

If the wielder has experienced a profound personal loss — a loved one, a home, a way of life — the hammer resonates with that grief. In this case, the psychic bonus damage increases to **1d8**, and the sorrow save DC increases to **18**. The hammer knows your grief because it is made of grief. It does not exploit it. It shares it.

While holding Lament, you have advantage on saving throws against being frightened. What is fear, compared to grief you have already survived? Additionally, you are immune to effects that would force you to feel specific emotions against your will — the hammer's sorrow fills that space so completely that no outside feeling can gain purchase.

## Awakened State

Speak the name of what you have lost most deeply — say it aloud for the first time — and the hammer's grief becomes contagious. As a bonus action, you speak the true name: the name of the person, place, or thing whose loss defines you. You have never said this name aloud before. You say it now. The hammer's head cracks along the grey tear-track veins, and from the fissures pours a visible aura of grey light — not shadow, but light that has been wept through. This state lasts for **1 minute**. Once invoked, the blade cannot be awakened again until you finish a long rest.

While awakened, the hammer's grief radiates outward in an **Aura of Crushing Sadness** extending 20 feet from you. The aura moves with you. Creatures of your choice within the aura have disadvantage on attack rolls and on Wisdom saving throws. The despair is physical — creatures that cast spells with verbal components while in the aura must make a **DC 17 Constitution saving throw** or the words catch in their throat, wasting the spell slot.

On a hit while awakened, Lament deals **1d8 bludgeoning plus 2d8 psychic damage**. When a creature fails its sorrow save, the effect intensifies: in addition to disadvantage on attacks, the creature falls prone, unable to muster the will to remain standing. At the end of each of its turns, a prone creature may repeat the Wisdom saving throw, ending the prone condition on a success but remaining overwhelmed regardless.

At the start of each of your turns, you can choose a single creature within the aura to bear the full focus of the hammer's grief — a **Grief-Sight**. The creature sees a vision of the thing it would most grieve to lose, vivid and terrible and happening right now. The creature must make a **DC 17 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, the creature is incapacitated until the start of your next turn as it processes the vision. Once a creature has succeeded on this save, it is immune to your Grief-Sight for the remainder of the awakening.

Once per awakening, as an action, you can raise the hammer and release a **Keening Wail** — a 30-foot cone of raw, audible grief. Each creature in the cone must make a **DC 17 Wisdom saving throw**, taking **8d8 psychic damage** on a failure and half as much on a success. A creature that fails the save is also incapacitated until the end of your next turn. The wail is the sound the god made when the mortal died. It has never been heard by anyone else. It will never be forgotten by anyone who hears it now.

---

## The True Name

The true name of Lament is not a word given by the blade — it is a word the wielder already knows but has never spoken aloud: the name of the person, place, or thing whose loss wounds them most deeply. The blade does not choose this name. The wielder does. To discover that the name has power, the wielder must simply say it while holding the hammer — but they must mean it, utterly and without reservation. The blade will know if the grief is genuine or performative. Many wielders have held Lament and never awakened it, not because they didn't know the name, but because they weren't ready to admit — even to themselves — what they had lost.
