# The Narrator's Quill
*Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
A long white feather quill — not from any bird known to naturalists, but from something that was mostly bird and partly page. The shaft is translucent, and if you hold it to the light, you can see ink flowing inside it, though you have never dipped it. The quill writes without a pot. The nib touches paper and leaves words the color of whatever emotion you were feeling when you wrote them.

Once per day, you may use the quill to write a single sentence — no more than twenty-five words — about something that will happen in the next minute. The sentence must be plausible. It must not contradict established facts. It must not directly cause harm or death (you cannot write "the king dies," but you can write "the king stumbles"). If the sentence meets these conditions, reality leans into it. The quill does not rewrite the world — it edits the margins.

## Properties

**Write the Next Minute (1/Long Rest).** As an action, write a single sentence of up to 25 words describing an event that will occur within the next 60 seconds. The sentence is subject to the following rules:

- It must be plausible in context ("I find a secret door" in a room that could reasonably have one; not "I find a secret door" in an open field).
- It must not directly kill, incapacitate, or deal damage to any creature. "The guard falls asleep" is acceptable. "The guard dies" is not.
- It must not contradict established facts. If the DM has stated there is no chandelier, you cannot write "the chandelier falls."
- It must not create objects of significant value, sentient creatures, or permanent structures.
- It can influence probability, environment, and minor narrative details.

The DM adjudicates whether the sentence is valid. If it is, the described event occurs naturally within the next minute — the guard sneezes at an opportune moment, the wind blows the curtains aside to reveal a hidden passage, the rope you tied holds just long enough. The quill does not force reality; it nudges probability toward your sentence's outcome. If the sentence is invalid or impossible, the action is wasted.

**Authorial Tone (passive).** The quill's ink reflects your emotional state. This has no mechanical effect, but it makes every document you write emotionally legible to anyone who reads it. You cannot lie in writing with this quill — the ink will shift to the color of guilt, and the words will tremble slightly. This is not a curse; the quill simply believes that writing should be honest.

**Editorial Privilege (1/Week).** Once per week, when a creature you can see makes an ability check, attack roll, or saving throw that you believe was narratively unsatisfying — a critical miss that ruined a heroic moment, a save that ended a dramatic scene too early — you may use your reaction to grant that creature advantage on the roll retroactively. The creature rerolls and uses the higher result. The quill emits a soft chime, and everyone present has the distinct feeling that the scene has been revised for pacing.

## Balance Notes
A reality-warping item gated by creativity and DM adjudication. Write the Next Minute is as powerful as the player's imagination and as limited as the DM's sense of plausibility — it's a collaborative storytelling tool disguised as a magic item. The "no harm" clause prevents it from being a combat nuke, but clever players will find enormous utility (escape routes, environmental advantages, social engineering). The Authorial Tone passive is pure flavor that has genuine RP consequences. Editorial Privilege is a once-per-week dramatic rescue button — powerful but infrequent enough to feel special. Compare to a *luck blade* (legendary, grants *wish*) — the quill is far more limited but also rechargeable. Very rare is appropriate.

## Variants
- **Rare — The Journalist's Quill:** Write the Next Minute affects the next 1 round (6 seconds), not 1 minute. Editorial Privilege 1/month. Cost: ~180,000 soul stones.
- **Legendary — The Author's Quill:** Write the Next Minute 3/long rest. The "no harm" clause is removed — you can write sentences that deal damage or incapacitate, but the DM may require the sentence to be longer or more specific to justify the effect. Editorial Privilege 1/long rest. Cost: ~5,000,000 soul stones.
