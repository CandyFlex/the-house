# Liar's Inkwell
*Wondrous item, rare (requires attunement)*

---

## Description
A squat inkwell of black glass, its rim chipped in three places, its interior swirling with ink that shifts color depending on the angle of the light — deep indigo, then violet, then a brief, unsettling crimson that vanishes before you can be sure you saw it. The inkwell is always full. You have never seen anyone refill it. You are not sure you want to know where the ink comes from.

When you dip a quill into the inkwell and write, the words that appear on the page are the logical negation of what you intended. If you write "I will pay you one hundred gold," the page reads "I will not pay you one hundred gold." If you write "The door is locked," it reads "The door is unlocked." The inkwell does not lie — it anti-lies. It tells a truth so precise that it can only be expressed as the exact opposite of a lie.

But here is the dangerous part: any agreement, contract, or promise written with this ink becomes magically binding — on its own reversed terms. If someone signs a Liar's Ink contract believing it says one thing, they are bound to the opposite. The inkwell does not consider this theft. It considers it editorial assistance.

## Properties

**Anti-Verity Ink (at will).** When you write with ink from this inkwell, the text that appears on the page is the logical negation of what you intended to write. You cannot control this effect — it applies to every word. The effect is semantic, not literal: "I will protect you" becomes "I will harm you," not "I will not not protect you." The inkwell is intelligent enough to understand double negatives, implications, and subtext. It has an INT of 20 for the purpose of interpreting your intended meaning.

**Binding Reversal (1/Long Rest).** Once per long rest, you may activate the inkwell's binding property by pressing your thumb to the inkwell's base while another creature signs a document written with its ink. The signing creature must succeed on a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw or be bound to the opposite of what they believe they are agreeing to. The binding persists for 30 days or until the terms are fulfilled. A creature bound in this way takes 4d6 psychic damage if they knowingly act against the reversed terms, and they intuitively understand what the reversed terms require of them (though not that the terms are reversed). Creatures with truesight or that can see through illusions have advantage on the save.

**Ink Supply.** The inkwell magically refills at each dawn. It holds enough ink for approximately 5 pages of writing per day.

## Balance Notes
A social manipulation item with teeth. The Anti-Verity Ink is pure RP gold — negotiating with fey, writing letters that mean the opposite, creating puzzles. The Binding Reversal is the combat/social payoff: trick an enemy into signing a "peace treaty" that actually binds them to hostility against their own allies. The 30-day duration and 4d6 psychic damage mirror *geas* (5th level, 30 days, 5d10 psychic), but the inkwell's version is more conditional (only on knowing violation) and requires setup (getting someone to sign). The DC 16 is appropriate for a rare item. The anti-verity property has no mechanical combat use — it's a flavor and puzzle tool — which keeps the item from being overpowered.

## Variants
- **Uncommon — Contradiction Quill:** A quill pre-loaded with 3 doses of anti-verity ink. No Binding Reversal. Refills at dawn. Cost: ~40,000 soul stones.
- **Very Rare — The Absolute Inkwell:** Binding Reversal 3/long rest, DC 18. The ink can rewrite existing text (1/page, 1/long rest), reversing its meaning retroactively. Cost: ~800,000 soul stones.
