# Wonders of the 1st Floor

*Ten items that should not exist. Each one is a small rebellion against the way things are supposed to work — gravity, time, memory, truth, direction. They share no history, no creator, no theme. They are united only by their refusal to make sense. They are the reason the 1st Floor's reality check sometimes fails.*

## Design Philosophy

These items were designed against type. D&D magic items typically fall into predictable buckets: weapons that deal more damage, armor that raises AC, wands that cast spells. The Wonders break that mold. Each one asks "what if the laws of reality had a sense of humor?" and answers with an item that is:

- **Uniquely mechanical.** No two Wonders use the same mechanic. They introduce time-debt, emotional imprinting, negation-writing, echo-capture, folding-as-summoning — mechanics that don't exist anywhere else.
- **RP-forward.** Most Wonders have no direct combat damage. Their power is narrative: the Compass eliminates possibilities, the Stone exposes hidden emotions, the Inkwell rewrites contracts.
- **Consequence-laden.** The most powerful Wonders (Puzzle-Box, Thimble, Hallway) come with built-in drawbacks or unpredictability. Power is borrowed, not owned.
- **Absurd but grounded.** A floating plum that makes fart noises is silly — but it's also a tactically useful flight item with a 300-foot AoE laughter effect. The absurdity is the delivery mechanism for genuine utility.
