# Session Script — 2026-04-26

**Party:** Ayo (Bladesinger), Algor (Illusionist), Gideon (Half-Elf Fighter/Monk), Elrigger (Human Fighter), Ject (Phantom Rogue), Malgus
**Absent:** Visha (held by Flesh — follow-up thread)

---

## SCALE REFERENCE
*(DM eyes only — internalize before session)*

- **The Institute** — Six towers, 40 to 110 feet tall, main footprint roughly 180 feet across. One imposing building. Not a campus.
- **The Grounds** — Approximately 300 feet of courtyard extending from the walls. This is the bubble the Gatemark registers.
- **The Former Capital** — The broader area Caesar centered his mirror kingdom in. Roughly 1–2 miles across. Emptying now.
- **Floor 1** — Continent-scale. Outposts are spread across it. Each is a significant expedition in permanent dark.
- **The Darkness** — Complete. The Institute is the only consistent light source on the floor visible from a distance.

---

## SCENE 1 — THE AFTERMATH

*Party is in Caesar's former establishment. He's dead. Vancent took the power plume. The False Hydra is gone — nothing remaining. Amarant and Vancent have left.*

> The air in here smells wrong. Not rot — something cleaner than rot and emptier. The particular absence of something that had been present so long you stopped noticing it until it left. The panels on the walls are dark. Not switched off — evacuated. Whatever ran this place ran on Caesar. It is not running on anything now.

*Let them look around, try to find anything useful from Caesar or his remains.*

> What's left of Caesar is going fast. Not decomposing. Radiating. You can see it in the air if you know what you're looking for — a fine dispersal outward, like the power was always borrowed and the lender just closed all accounts at once. The organs, the connections, everything tied to him: releasing. Not storing. Dispersing into nothing.

*If they try to take anything — it crumbles, radiates away, or is already gone. Nothing is recoverable here.*

> Outside, through the opening — the sky is wrong. The Suns that Caesar strung across it like a private solar system are going out. You can watch it happen if you look.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE SUNS FALL

*Let this breathe. One by one. Then the last.*

> They go out in sequence. Some fast — a flare and then nothing, the way a candle goes when you pinch it. Some slow — dimming over minutes, trailing off like they weren't sure. Each one that dies takes a section of the sky with it.

*Pause between each. Let the players feel the dark accumulating.*

> The last one goes.
>
> For a moment there is nothing. Not the old twilight this floor used to have — that was always soft, always evening. This is different. Full dark. The kind with weight to it, the kind that makes you suddenly aware of how much sound you'd been filtering out.
>
> The carrier creatures are loud in it.

*Beat. Let them sit in the complete dark.*

> The darkness smells like cold stone and something organic at the edges — the carrier creatures active now in a way they weren't before, closer, patterns you can hear moving around the perimeter of wherever you're standing.

---

## SCENE 3 — THE TRANSFORMATION

*They turn back toward the structure.*

> It isn't what it was.
>
> What stood there before — the angular geometry, the dark solar surfaces, the structure that felt like the inside of something that didn't like you — is gone.
>
> In its place: stone. Dark stone, not quite one color at any angle, the kind that holds something deeper in it depending on how the light catches. Six towers of unequal height, each leaning very slightly in a different direction, as if they were built across different centuries by hands that weren't comparing notes. Tall narrow windows, pointed at the top. Light coming through them — not warm exactly, not cold, the particular quality of light that lives in corridors that have seen a lot of use.
>
> It is the only light on the floor. From anywhere with line of sight, this is what there is.

*Look at Ayo. Say nothing. Wait.*

---

## SCENE 4 — R.C.'s REVEAL

*A voice, from nowhere visible:*

**R.C.:** *"The cobblework on the fourth tower is weight-bearing. I'd recommend against the window on the east face until someone looks at the lintel."*

*Nobody there. Let them look.*

**R.C.:** *"The Gatemark is already running. You registered when she walked through. You should know that."*

*More looking. More voices. Let it go on. R.C. keeps offering occasional observations — about the building, about the floor, about nothing in particular. The party searches, looks in doorways, looks upward.*

*When someone finally looks directly at the rock — or when you've let it go long enough:*

> It's the size of a large fist. Smooth, dark, worn to a shine across most of its surface — the kind of smooth that takes centuries to achieve, not craft. There are traces of something carved into it, old enough that the edges have rounded into suggestions. It is sitting on the ground as if it has always been sitting there.
>
> Two eyes, if you are close enough: black, the size of apple seeds, catching the light in a way that suggests depth. A slit below them, barely visible, moving slightly when the voice comes again.

**R.C.:** *"I've been waiting here since before the Calamity. I have seen considerably more notable things than your expressions right now."*

---

## SCENE 5 — THE INSTITUTE TOUR

*R.C. walks them through. He moves by rolling with intent — not fast, not labored, just purposeful in a way that's immediately apparent for a rock.*

---

### THE GATEMARK

> The arch is taller than it needs to be. The stone of it is a slightly different material from the rest of the wall — older, or worked differently, the surface of it finer. There is no door. Walking through has a quality like a held breath. Not threatening. Attentive. As if something just noticed you and wrote it down.

**R.C.:** *"Anything that passes through is recorded. It doesn't make judgments. It counts."*

---

### THE HALL OF MERIDIAN

> Vaulted ceiling, dark stone ribbing following the curve up to a height that makes the room feel geological. The light comes from the walls themselves — veins of pale crystal running through the stone, lit from within, casting cold blue-white that doesn't flicker. The floor is unmarked grey-black stone. No decoration. No seats. The proportions of somewhere built to establish something.
>
> It smells like cut stone and something older beneath — the mineral smell of a space that has been sealed and unsealed over very long periods of time.

**R.C.:** *"This is where you speak from, when you need to speak to the floor. The room carries your voice further than you expect."*

---

### THE SCRIPTORIUM OF MARKS

> A long room. Lower ceiling than the Meridian — intimate by comparison, the kind of scale that makes you lean toward things rather than back from them. Surfaces line the walls floor to ceiling. Caesar's administrative records are already dying on the upper sections, going dark the way something goes dark when it loses its source. But beneath those, cut directly into the stone: symbols. Dense. Small. Covering every surface that Caesar's material hasn't obscured. Codes, registration marks, some of them still carrying faint light.
>
> It smells like paper and cold mineral and dried ink that has been drying for a very long time.

*R.C. rolls to the far wall. Stops. Reads.*

**R.C.:** *"The carvings predate the last three people who held this floor. Someone maintained a secondary record in case the formal system was destroyed."*

*He doesn't say anything else. He keeps reading.*

---

### THE WATCH

> The stairs narrow as they climb. By the top, barely shoulder-wide. The platform at the summit has no rail — open air on all sides, the floor spread below in complete darkness.
>
> In that darkness, things read differently than they do in light. Heat sources. Soul-energy. Movement patterns. The carrier creatures move in long arcing paths below. Points of light cluster in several directions at distance — fires, groups, something gathering. The floor is enormous. The Institute, from here, is a point.
>
> The wind up here smells like the darkness below — cold stone, something animal at the edges, the faint metallic note of soul energy dispersing without direction.

**R.C.** *(if carried up — he can't climb stairs):* *"What you're looking at is approximately what the floor holds now. It is less than it was. The movement patterns you're seeing at the edges are people who don't know yet what happened."*

---

### THE CHAMBER OF APPOINTMENT

> Circular room. One marking on the floor — a glyph, cut deep, the edges worn smooth by age into curves but the shape still legible. The walls are bare. No seats. No decoration. The function of the room is singular and apparent.
>
> The stone here is colder than the rest of the building. Dry cold — not damp — the cold of something that has been waiting in one state for a very long time. Your breath is visible.

**R.C.:** *"This is where the floor recognizes what you give it."* — *He pauses.* *"The previous owner used this room. The residue of that is still in the glyph. It is worth knowing before you use it."*

---

### THE LONG GALLERY

> A corridor that runs the full second level and does not seem to account for the exterior dimensions of the building. On the walls: carved relief panels, embedded materials, painted sections in styles that shift as you move along it. Moving from the entrance toward the far end is moving backward in time.
>
> The recent sections — Caesar's era — are wrong the way a portrait goes wrong when you learn what the subject became. What was rendered in triumph reads as something else now. Moving past those, the style changes. Older materials, different hands. A forest in permanent twilight. People who don't look like anyone you've encountered on this floor. A sky without Suns — just the old dark, full and soft.

*R.C. stops at a specific panel near the far end and looks at it.*

*He does not explain it unless asked directly. If asked:*

**R.C.:** *"That is what this floor was before."* — *He doesn't elaborate.*

---

### THE PRACTICE COURTS

> Four levels, each accessed from a different floor of the main structure. The uppermost: inscribed with sigils pressed into the stone in a graduated pattern, a methodology still legible if studied, predating Caesar entirely. The floor of it is clean. The air here is still and focused.
>
> The lowest level: raw. The floor impact-scored and cracked in patterns made by things being fought against it. The walls scorched in irregular shapes. The smell of old exertion — stone dust and something that didn't want to stop.

**R.C.:** *"Someone taught here, before. The upper court's system is recoverable. The lower court's history is less recoverable."*

---

### THE UNDERCROFT

> Below the main floor. The air changes immediately upon descent — thicker, cooler, carrying the smell of active systems, something between old metal and the particular charge before a storm. The panels here are lit, some of them, running processes in materials that aren't quite stone and aren't quite metal.
>
> Two systems are visible in the same space, layered. One reads clearly — active, recent. One is dimmer, running underneath it in patterns designed for a different registrant. Both are doing things. Both are real.

**R.C.:** *"The previous registration isn't gone. It has receded. The longer the floor runs under the new one, the more it recedes. This is not a fast process."*

---

### THE DESCENT *(stairs only — do not enter)*

> The stairs going down from the Undercroft change material partway. The building's stone gives way to something older — rougher, or shaped by something other than tools. The air from below is noticeably colder. Damp cold, this time. No light source is visible from the top of the stairs. The smell coming up is stone and something older than stone — earth, roots, something that has been sealed down there for a very long time.

**R.C.** *(without being asked):* *"Not tonight."*

*If they push:*

**R.C.:** *"That is pre-Calamity foundation. Most of what is down there has never been studied because people who wanted to study it did not survive long enough to report. Tonight is not the night."*

## SCENE 6 — WHAT R.C. TELLS THEM

*After the tour, when they ask what they need, what this costs, how this works.*

---

**On Caesar's power — when they ask:**

**R.C.:** *"What Vancent took when he demolished him was the significant portion. The remainder — the organs, the connections, the loyalists who carried pieces of him — is dispersing. Caesar's relationship with power was extractive by design. He gave surplus to those he connected to so that he could take it back on demand. His death activated the mechanism involuntarily. The ones who held his organs inside them have been dying since the moment he did. There is nothing recoverable. The floor will absorb the dispersal over time. You are not in a position to intercept it."*

---

**On what the floor needs — when they ask:**

**R.C.:** *"The registration is held. The administrative layer acknowledges the new holder. What is not yet held is the floor itself. That is a significant difference."*

**R.C.:** *"The Chamber of Appointment requires a willing candidate of sufficient weight. Not a title. A person — someone the floor will recognize as load-bearing. You install that person as an Hour, and the floor holds its connection to the registration even when the holder is absent. Without one, the registration weakens the moment the holder leaves. Add more, the hold strengthens. That is the minimum."*

**R.C.:** *"Beyond that: the floor's economy is broken. Caesar managed the soul energy through the Suns — consuming it from above and redistributing it. With the Suns gone, soul energy will begin to accumulate in the floor again. Slowly, and unevenly. You will be resource-poor for some time."*

**R.C.:** *"The Undercroft needs to be resolved. Until it is, this floor responds to two registrations. Things Caesar built here still respond to the older one. Some doors. Some creatures. Some locations you haven't found yet."*

---

**On the outpost system — when they ask about the floor or where to go:**

**R.C.:** *"There were twelve outposts. One for each month. Each was an independent infrastructure node — power, registration anchor, population. Caesar took the floor by destroying them. He wanted the energy consolidated under him rather than distributed."*

**R.C.:** *"I watched eleven of them fall. One I did not watch. Outpost July — it was already marginal before Caesar moved on it. He may have considered it beneath attention. I cannot tell you what you will find there."*

**R.C.:** *"Of the eleven I watched — three were not fully destroyed. Their holders went silent or fled rather than fight to the end. If the holders survived, the outpost may still carry something. I can tell you where they were. I cannot tell you what is there now."*

---

## SCENE 7 — THE DIRECTION

*R.C. gives them three locations. These are the options. Let them choose.*

---

**OUTPOST JULY**
> *"Southeast from here. A significant distance — several days in this dark, moving carefully. The last time I had sight of it, it was running at minimal capacity. Someone was maintaining it, or had been. I do not know who."*

*Known surviving. Decrepit. Lowest risk, lowest unknown. Something is keeping the lights on.*

---

**OUTPOST [MONTH — YOUR CALL]**
> *"Further. Northwest. The holder there did not fight Caesar — she went silent the day he moved on her outpost. I do not know if silence meant survival or simply that she died quietly. The outpost's infrastructure was intact when Caesar's forces arrived. Whether they took it fully or left it when she stopped responding, I cannot say."*

*Unknown condition. A person may still be there. The infrastructure may be intact.*

---

**OUTPOST [MONTH — YOUR CALL]**
> *"The one Caesar wanted most. He took it by force, not neglect — something there was worth taking. But the records I could observe suggested he never fully incorporated what it held into his own system. Either it resisted integration or he never finished the process. It has been quiet since. Something may still be in it that Caesar couldn't absorb."*

*Highest risk. Highest potential reward. Caesar wanted what's there and couldn't fully take it.*

---

*Let them discuss. Let them choose a direction. The session ends when they commit.*

## R.C. — VOICE GUIDE

- **Pace:** Unhurried. He has been waiting since before the Calamity. He is not in a hurry now.
- **Register:** Factual. He states things. He does not editorialize unless the editorialism is very dry.
- **What he volunteers:** Structural observations, historical context, warnings about specific things. Relevant information, often slightly adjacent to what was asked.
- **What he withholds:** His full name. What he saw during the Calamity. The specific contents of the Descent. Anything he doesn't think they're ready to use yet.
- **His tell:** When something matters to him, he goes quieter rather than louder. Silence from R.C. is more significant than his words.
- **On friendship:** He will not initiate it. He will not refuse it if it arrives. He doesn't believe it will arrive, but this belief is not bitterness — it is a working model of the world that he has not yet had reason to revise.
