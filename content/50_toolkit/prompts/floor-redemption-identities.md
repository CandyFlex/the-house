# Phase 2 — THE SHAPE OF A NEW FLOOR

*The player picks ONE. This is not a finished civilization. It is a direction — the general character of the place that is forming, the feeling of walking through it, the kind of people it attracts and the kind of lives they're building. Nothing here has been named yet. The inhabitants are still figuring out what to call things. What's described below is the texture, not the terminology.*

*What all five share: the MMO infrastructure is visible and interactive — teleport gates, quest markers, respawn logic. It hums in the background of daily life. The people who live here accept it the way you accept weather. The dungeons are present and dangerous and valuable. Outsiders from other floors come here seeking something — refuge, neutrality, a fresh start, a place to wager something they couldn't wager at home. Floor 1 was never a destination before. Now it is.*


## 1

Imagine arriving somewhere that was clearly built with care. The roads are laid with intention. The buildings have proportion. The light falls in a way that feels deliberate, like someone adjusted it. Everything suggests that this place was made by someone who wanted it to be good.

The people who live here carry themselves with a quiet confidence — not pride exactly, more like the comfort of someone who believes the world around them is reliable. They're drawn to things that reinforce that feeling: clear structures, visible systems, a sense that things work the way they're supposed to. When something new appears — a quest marker that wasn't there yesterday, a teleport gate that shifted its destination — they respond with curiosity rather than alarm. They want to understand it. They assume it makes sense, even if they haven't figured out how yet.

For outsiders arriving from other floors, this place offers something rare: stability. The world doesn't shift under your feet here. The rules are visible. The people will explain how things work without demanding you already know. Refugees gravitate toward settlements where the infrastructure is clearest, where the teleport routes are well-marked and the quests are straightforward. They come here to catch their breath, to figure out what they're going to do next, to be somewhere that doesn't require them to already understand it.

There is a wager built into this place, though it isn't spoken of directly. The stability is real, but it's also fragile. The world is still new. The systems that feel so reliable haven't been tested. When you settle here, when you build a life here, you're betting that the order will hold. Most people don't think about what happens if it doesn't. The ones who do don't talk about it.

> *Direction: ordered, deliberate, reliable. Draws people who want structure. The wager is on permanence.*


## 2

Imagine arriving somewhere that was once magnificent and is now something else. The grandeur is still present — great arches, vaulted ceilings, stonework that speaks of a time when someone spared no expense — but it's been worn down. Moss in the joints. Roots in the walls. Sections of ceiling open to sky. The place is a ruin, but it's a ruin people live in. Candles burn in windows whose upper floors collapsed centuries ago, or last week, and no one is quite sure which.

The people who live here are comfortable with unanswerable questions. They don't need to know where the ruins came from. They don't need to agree on what the dungeons are. They live in the space between what was and what is, and they treat that space as home. They're drawn to things with history, with layers, with the sense that something happened here before they arrived and left its mark. When something shifts — a dungeon entrance that moved, a ruin that settled — they adapt. They always have.

For outsiders, this place offers obscurity. It's easy to disappear here. The ruins are vast, the population is scattered, and nobody asks too many questions about where you came from. Refugees who don't want to be found come here. So do people who have done things they're running from. The place doesn't judge. It has seen worse. Everyone here has seen worse.

The wager here is on whether the past matters. Some people believe the ruins hold answers — that if you dig deep enough, explore thoroughly enough, you'll find out what happened and why and what comes next. Others believe the past is just the past and digging won't change anything. Both groups are betting something. The diggers are betting their time, their safety, sometimes their sanity. The ones who leave the ruins alone are betting that ignorance is safer. Neither bet has been settled.

> *Direction: ruined, layered, accepting. Draws people who want to disappear. The wager is on whether understanding the past matters.*


## 3

Imagine arriving somewhere that doesn't apologize for being hard. The cold gets into your bones. The buildings are thick. The roads are the ones that survived, not the ones someone planned. Everything here is practical — iron hinges, heavy doors, stone walls that were built to last through winters that last longer than they should.

The people who live here are direct. They'll tell you what works and what doesn't. They'll help you if you're willing to work. They don't care where you came from — they care whether you can carry your weight, whether you'll hold up your end, whether you'll be useful when things go wrong. They're drawn to things that are proven: reliable tools, tested methods, people who've been here long enough to know what they're doing. New things are treated with suspicion until they prove themselves.

For outsiders, this place offers a kind of honesty that's hard to find elsewhere. Nobody will lie to you about how things are. Nobody will promise you it's going to be okay. What they'll do is show you how to survive, if you're willing to learn. Refugees arrive broken and the place doesn't fix them, but it gives them work, and for some people, work is closer to healing than comfort ever was.

The wager here is on what you're willing to give up. The place demands pragmatism. It rewards it. But pragmatism has limits. When something impossible happens — when a dungeon spits out something that shouldn't exist, when the rules that have always held suddenly don't — the practical response is to treat it as a new resource or a new threat. Use it. Avoid it. Move on. But some things can't be used without being changed by them. Some people learn this too late.

> *Direction: hard, honest, practical. Draws people who want to earn their place. The wager is on what you sacrifice to survive.*


## 4

Imagine arriving somewhere that feels alive in a way that's difficult to describe. The colors are slightly too vivid. Sounds carry further than they should. The boundary between what's real and what's felt has never been clearly drawn here, and after a while you stop trying to draw it. The roads follow lines that weren't laid by anyone — natural currents, the shape of the land, paths that emerged because they needed to. Buildings incorporate living things: trees that grow through roofs, moss that insulates walls, gardens that weren't planted but simply appeared and stayed.

The people who live here accept mystery as a condition of existence. They don't agree on what the world is, and they consider this normal. They're drawn to things that evade easy explanation — phenomena, patterns, the way the wind sometimes forms almost-words. They don't try to solve the world. They live in it and see what happens. When a dungeon entrance appears where it wasn't yesterday, they mark it and move on. When the quest markers rearrange themselves, they adapt their routes.

For outsiders, this place offers permission to stop understanding. Not everyone who arrives is running from danger. Some are running from certainty — from worlds where everything is known and explained and accounted for. They come here to be somewhere that doesn't demand answers. Refugees arrive and find that no one asks who they were. No one asks what they believe. The only question that matters is whether they can live alongside strangeness without trying to tame it.

The wager here is on what you let in. The place is open — to people, to strangeness, to whatever wants to exist here. That openness is the point. But some things that come through aren't benign. Some things that seem beautiful at first glance are patient in ways that should worry you. The people who live here know this. They accept it. The wager is that openness is worth the risk. So far, it has been.

> *Direction: strange, open, accepting. Draws people seeking mystery or fleeing certainty. The wager is on whether openness invites harm.*


## 5

Imagine arriving somewhere where everything is visible. The teleport gates hum at intersections and their schedules are posted. The quest markers float at shoulder height and anyone can read them. The systems that run this place don't hide themselves — they're infrastructure, like roads or waterways, and the people interact with them with the fluency of long practice.

The people who live here are oriented around understanding. They want to know how things work — not out of devotion or fear, but out of a conviction that knowledge is leverage. Children learn to parse quest markers alongside their letters. Specialists devote their lives to studying specific systems: teleport routing, marker patterns, the logic behind which dungeons appear where and when. There is a competitiveness to it, but it's friendly, mostly. The satisfaction comes from figuring things out.

For outsiders, this place offers opportunity. The systems are transparent. If you're willing to learn, you can compete. It doesn't matter who you were on another floor. What matters is whether you can read the markers and move fast enough. Refugees arrive with nothing and discover that the interface doesn't care about their past. Some thrive. Some don't. The ones who thrive are the ones who treat the place like a language to be learned.

The wager here is on mastery. The people who know the systems best have the most power, the most wealth, the most influence. But the systems change. Dungeons evolve. Markers appear for quests that don't exist. Teleport gates shift without warning. Everyone here is betting that they can keep up — that their understanding is deep enough to adapt when the rules change. The ones who bet wrong don't just lose. They stop mattering.

> *Direction: transparent, competitive, learnable. Draws people who want to prove themselves through skill. The wager is on whether mastery can survive change.*


---

*Five directions. Different feels, different draws, different wagers. The player picks one. Then we figure out what the mechanism looks like — how Floor 1 actually functions as a neutral ground, how outsiders arrive, what systems govern their stay.*
