# Phase 2 — AESTHETIC THEMES

*The player picks ONE. It determines the entire floor's visual identity — from the countryside to the city gates to how dungeon entrances present themselves. Dungeons themselves have unique internal themes, but their *interface* with the overworld matches the chosen aesthetic.*

*Each theme has 2-3 variants. Pick the variant that feels right, or blend between them.*

---

## THEME 1 — GILDED
*A world that works. Polished, orderly, designed. Architecture that communicates stability — stone keeps, arched bridges, cobblestone plazas, banners hanging from every lamp post. The kind of fantasy kingdom that feels like it's been running smoothly for centuries.*

### Variants:

**Gilded — Court**
The capital is the heart. A white-stone castle-city on terraced hills, connected by sweeping staircases and ivy-covered walls. Marble fountains in every square. The outer city is walled districts, each with its own market and temple. Countryside is ordered farmland — checkerboard fields, walled vineyards, ancient roads connecting walled market towns. Hexatons are crossroads villages with stone inns and central wells. Dungeon entrances present as sealed crypts beneath standing stones, hidden vault doors in cliff faces, or iron grates in ancient tower basements — formal, deliberate, meant to be found.
*Palette: Warm gold, sky blue, cream, sage.*

**Gilded — Trade**
The market is the heart. The capital is a river city — canals, wharfs, merchant quarters stacked against the water. Guildhalls with stained glass. Stone bridges lined with stalls. Outer city spills beyond the walls — timber-frame warehouses, ropewalks, shipyards. Countryside is trade roads connecting fortified market towns. Hexatons are coaching inns and waystations a day's ride apart. Dungeon entrances present as sealed guild vaults, smuggler tunnels under canal houses, or old trade routes that collapsed and revealed something below — connected to the economy.
*Palette: Terracotta, river-stone grey, brass, indigo.*

**Gilded — March**
The frontier is the heart. A borderland city — thick walls, watchtowers, garrisons. Military architecture softened by generations of habitation. Outer city is a shantytown-turned-permanent, timber construction with slate roofs. Countryside is fortified villages, signal towers on hilltops, walled farms. Hexatons are blockhouses with a tavern and a stable. Dungeon entrances present as old watchtowers that sank into the earth, sealed siege tunnels, or caves beneath hillforts — the frontier has layers of conflict buried in it.
*Palette: Iron grey, faded gold, ochre, pine green.*

---

## THEME 2 — EMBER
*A world that remembers. Warm light against stone and timber. Architecture built for protection — thick walls, narrow windows, high ceilings. The light comes from within — hearths, forges, braziers. This world is not grim, but it is serious. It knows what it survived.*

### Variants:

**Ember — Hearth**
The hall is the heart. A great keep-city, built around a central feasting hall that never lets its fire die. The city radiates outward in rings — inner ring is stone manors and temples, outer ring is timber-frame workshops and homes connected by covered walkways. Countryside is scattered longhouses and steading-farms, each a day's ride apart, each with its own hearth. Hexatons are single-hall villages — a long building that serves as inn, meeting house, and shelter. Dungeon entrances present as sealed crypts beneath the hall's foundation, barrows in the hills marked by standing stones, or old hearths that still burn with a different kind of fire. The warmth above ground contrasts with what sleeps below.
*Palette: Warm amber, aged oak, hearth-glow orange, slate grey.*

**Ember — Forge**
The smithy is the heart. A city built around industry — smelters, foundries, armories. Ironwork everywhere — gates, railings, lampposts forged into art. Stone buildings with copper roofs, glowing chimney stacks. Outer city is workshops and forges in terraced rows. Countryside is mining towns, quarry settlements, charcoal-burner camps in the forest. Hexatons are mill-villages — watermills, windmills, fulling mills — each with a smithy. Dungeon entrances present as sealed mine shafts, collapsed smelters that opened a void below, or forge-chambers where the heat never died. The fire that shaped the surface also shaped what lies beneath.
*Palette: Burnished copper, charcoal, ember orange, rust red.*

**Ember — Citadel**
The sanctuary is the heart. A walled city on high ground — cathedral, cloisters, scriptorium. The architecture is heavy, protective, sacred. Arched cloisters, bell towers, rose windows that catch the last light. Outer city is pilgrims' quarters and hospice grounds. Countryside is abbeys, monasteries, chapels at crossroads — each a refuge. Hexatons are wayside shrines with a single inn attached. Dungeon entrances present as sealed ossuaries beneath chapels, crypts that predate the buildings above, or passages behind altars that lead to older, stranger sanctuaries. The surface offers shelter. Below offers truth.
*Palette: Candle-wax cream, stone grey, stained-glass ruby, bronze.*

## THEME 3 — BLOOM
*A world that grows. Lush, abundant, fertile. Architecture that lives alongside nature rather than dominating it. Buildings have gardens on their roofs, vines on their walls, trees growing through their courtyards. This is a world that is actively, joyfully alive.*

### Variants:

**Bloom — Orchard**
The harvest is the heart. A city in a valley surrounded by fruit trees on every slope. Stone and warm wood, roofs covered in moss and flowering plants. Open-air markets in garden courtyards. Canals line the streets, clear water running. Outer city is orchard-terraces — homes among the trees. Countryside is endless fields of grain, wildflower meadows, hedge-lined lanes between farming villages. Hexatons are clusters of cottages around a mill, a well, and a fruit press in the center. Dungeon entrances present as hollow trees big enough to walk into, wells that go deeper than they should, or thickets that part to reveal stone stairs going down. The abundance above was built on something older.
*Palette: Emerald, soft pink, butter yellow, sky blue.*

**Bloom — Garden**
The cultivation is the heart. A terraced city on a hillside — every level is a garden. Flowers cascade from every balcony, vegetables grow on every roof. The architecture is light — latticed wood, white plaster, colorful tiles. Water features everywhere — fountains, rills, reflecting pools. Outer city is garden districts, each with a different character. Countryside is arranged like a designed landscape — copses, glades, ornamental bridges, wild areas that are actually curated. Hexatons are garden-villages — a green, a well, a communal oven. Dungeon entrances present as overgrown grottos, sunken gardens that reveal a ceiling below, or fountains that drain to reveal a stair. The surface is art. Below is nature before it was arranged.
*Palette: Cerulean, lavender, chartreuse, cream.*

**Bloom — Wilds**
The wilderness is the heart. The city is woven into an ancient forest — treehouses connected by rope bridges, halls built around living trunks, clearings that serve as markets. The forest doesn't stop at the city wall — it continues through it. Outer city is canopy settlements and root-level workshops. Countryside is old-growth forest with game trails, hidden glades, ruins swallowed by vegetation. Hexatons are clearings with a communal hearth and shelter — temporary, mobile, seasonal. Dungeon entrances present as caves behind waterfalls, roots that part to reveal a stone door, or animal dens that open into vast underground spaces. The wild above connects to the ancient below.
*Palette: Deep forest green, moss, mushroom cap, honey gold.*

---

## THEME 4 — SPIRE
*A world that reaches. Vertical, aspirational, open. Architecture on high ground — hilltops, plateaus, ridges. Towers connected by bridges. The eye is always drawn upward. This world is about height, light, and horizon.*

### Variants:

**Spire — Beacon**
The tower is the heart. A city on a plateau — white stone, silver roofs, spires that catch the first and last light. The center is a great tower — part lighthouse, part sanctuary, visible from everywhere on the floor. Wide avenues, open squares, bridges connecting elevated districts. Outer city terraces down the plateau slopes. Countryside is hilltop towns, each with its own smaller spire, visible to each other across the valleys. Hexatons are single-spire waystations — a tower, a stable, a well. Dungeon entrances present as sealed chambers beneath spires, wells that drop into lit caverns, or tower basements that extend far deeper than the tower rises. The height above was balanced by depth below.
*Palette: Pearl white, pale gold, cerulean, silver.*

**Spire — Archive**
The library is the heart. A city of knowledge — towers are stacks, plazas are reading rooms, bridges are lined with alcoves and scriptoria. Every surface that can hold writing does. The architecture is scholar-gothic — pointed arches, ribbed vaults, large windows. Outer city is scribe quarters and bookbinder districts. Countryside is monastery-towns and university-villages, each specializing in a field of knowledge. Hexatons are chapter houses with scriptoria attached. Dungeon entrances present as sealed archive vaults, spiral stairs behind bookcases, or forgotten reading rooms that were built over something older. The knowledge above was built on mystery below.
*Palette: Parchment cream, ink black, leather brown, silver.*

**Spire — Bastion**
The wall is the heart. A fortress-city on a ridge — concentric walls, gatehouses, towers at every interval. Not grim — proud. This city has never fallen. Architecture is military but beautiful — carved battlements, banner-lined walls, parade grounds with fountains. Outer city is a ring of fortified farms and training grounds. Countryside is signal-tower networks, fortified bridges, walled market towns at choke points. Hexatons are blockhouses with a beacon, a garrison of six, and a tavern. Dungeon entrances present as sally ports sealed from the inside, old siege tunnels the city grew over, or crypts beneath the oldest tower. The walls above were built on buried foundations.
*Palette: Limestone white, slate blue, bronze, pine.*

---

## THEME 5 — THORN
*A world that endures. Sturdy, practical, organic. Architecture that grows from the land rather than being imposed on it. Timber, stone, thatch. Nothing wasted, nothing decorative without purpose. This is a world built by people who use their hands.*

### Variants:

**Thorn — Timber**
The hall is the heart. A city of wood — great timber-frame buildings with carved beams, steep roofs, and long galleries. The architecture is honest — you can see how everything is built. The great hall is the center: meetings, feasts, judgments all happen under one roof. Outer city is craftsman quarters — woodworkers, weavers, potters. Countryside is scattered farmsteads connected by ancient drove roads. Hexatons are hamlets around a common green, a well, and a timber market hall. Dungeon entrances present as root-cellars that open into larger chambers, sealed barrows timber-lined and earthen-mounded, or old hall foundations that sank into the earth. The wood above came from forests that grew over older things.
*Palette: Weathered oak, iron grey, hearth orange, flax.*

**Thorn — Stone**
The hold is the heart. A city of stone — granite blocks, slate roofs, flagged floors. Built into a hillside or cliff face. Architecture is solid, permanent, unadorned but well-proportioned. The hold is the center — part fortress, part storehouse, part meeting hall. Outer city is stone cottages cascading down the slope, each built by its owner. Countryside is dry-stone-walled fields, stone barns, sheep pastures on high ground. Hexatons are stone hamlets around a communal barn and a spring. Dungeon entrances present as sealed stone cists, collapsed mines the earth swallowed, or springs that feed into flooded underground chambers. The stone above was quarried from caves below.
*Palette: Granite grey, slate blue, heather purple, sheep-white.*

**Thorn — Iron**
The crossroads is the heart. A city at the meeting of roads — not planned, but grown. Architecture is mixed — timber, stone, brick — whatever was available when each building went up. The center is a market square surrounded by guild halls and inns. The city is a shape made by trade. Outer city is straggling roadside development — smithies, tanneries, breweries on the edge. Countryside is hamlets every half-day's ride along the trade roads. Hexatons are crossroads taverns that grew into villages. Dungeon entrances present as sealed wellheads, cellar passages between buildings that connect to older networks, or crypts beneath guild halls. The traffic above was built on routes the dead also used.
*Palette: Brick red, charcoal, tan, iron grey.*

---

## THEME 6 — VEIL
*A world that holds secrets. Mist in the valleys, fog on the moors, half-glimpsed shapes in the periphery. Architecture is beautiful but elusive — you're never quite sure you saw what you think you saw. Not threatening. Mysterious. The kind of world where things are true even if they aren't real.*

### Variants:

**Veil — Hollow**
The glade is the heart. A city built in a great forest clearing — but the clearing is larger than it should be, and the forest never encroaches. Architecture is pale stone and silver wood, with many arches and open colonnades. Mist moves through the streets at certain hours — predictable, rhythmic. Outer city dissolves into the treeline — homes half-visible through the mist. Countryside is paths that seem to change when you're not looking, clearings that appear and disappear, bridges that only some people can cross. Hexatons are the kind of village you arrive at without remembering the journey. Dungeon entrances aren't hidden — they appear differently to different people. A door that wasn't there yesterday. A stair that goes down when you look away and back.
*Palette: Mist grey, silver, pale violet, bone white.*

**Veil — Mirror**
The reflection is the heart. A city built on a still lake — canals instead of streets, and every building has a twin beneath the water. The sky and the water mirror each other perfectly. Architecture is low and horizontal — bridges and pavilions connected by causeways. White plaster, dark timber, lanterns reflected doubly. Outer city is marshland settlements on stilts. Countryside is meres and fens connected by raised paths. Hexatons are island-villages — a cluster of buildings on a patch of dry ground. Dungeon entrances present as reflections that don't match — a stair in the water that has no equivalent above, or doors that only open on the reflection side. You enter through the wrong surface.
*Palette: Silver-blue, deep teal, lantern gold, mist white.*

**Veil — Echo**
The sound is the heart. A city built in a canyon — architecture carved into both walls, connected by bridges. Every sound echoes in a way that creates harmonics — the city sings at certain hours. Architecture is carved stone, warm lighting from within the rock. Outer city spreads along the canyon floor and rim. Countryside is valleys and gorges connected by natural bridges. Hexatons are clusters of dwellings around a resonant feature — a waterfall, a echo-chamber cave, a wind-carved arch. Dungeon entrances present as places where the echo doesn't return — a tunnel that absorbs sound instead of reflecting it, a chamber that should sing but stays silent. The surface is full of voice. Below is where sound goes to stop.
*Palette: Sandstone amber, shadow blue, copper, twilight pink.*

---

*That's six themes, each with 2-3 variants. The player picks ONE theme and ONE variant (or blends between variants of that theme). That one choice sets the entire overworld — outer city, inner city, countryside, hexatons, and how dungeon entrances interface with the surface.*
