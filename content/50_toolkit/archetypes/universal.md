# Archetypes — Universal

Stock types that recur across all floors and all settings. These are 
starting points, not definitions. The value is in the remix — take one 
of these and ask: "What does this look like on Floor 6? What does it 
look like after the faction it served betrayed it?"

---

## The Exiled Heir
Someone who was supposed to inherit something — a throne, a title, 
a responsibility — and didn't. Now they carry the shape of what they 
were supposed to be, and it's the most dangerous thing about them.

*The House version:* What does inheritance mean when "floor of origin" 
determines identity? What does it mean to be the heir of Floor 3 when 
Floor 3 has been sealed?

---

## The Dead God
A deity, force, or power that was once dominant and is now absent, 
dormant, or destroyed — but whose absence shapes everything. The dead 
god is still running the world through its corpse: institutions built 
in its image, people who still pray to it, the wound it left.

*The House version:* What is the mesophysical residue of a dead god? 
Does intention outlast the entity that had it?

---

## The Living Library
A person (or place, or object) that contains more knowledge than they 
can safely hold. They know things that would start wars. They've been 
keeping secrets for so long that the secrets have become their identity. 
They don't know who they'd be without the keeping.

*The House version:* What happens when a Living Library crosses floors? 
Do the secrets belong to the floor they were collected on?

---

## The Threshold Guardian
Someone or something that controls passage — between floors, between 
zones, between states of being. Not necessarily malevolent. May be 
neutral, bound by rules that predate them. The question is always: 
what is the toll, and who set it?

*The House version:* Floor transitions are the most powerful Threshold 
Guardian territory. Who controls the transitions? Are there unofficial 
transitions that the guardians don't know about?

---

## The Reluctant Prophet
Someone who has seen something they can't unsee — a truth about the 
world, a future event, a hidden structure — and is struggling with 
whether to tell anyone. Not struggling with the decision to lie, but 
with the decision to speak. The prophecy wants to come out. They're 
not sure what it will cost.

*The House version:* What counts as prophecy in a mesophysical world? 
If intention and matter are not fully separate, does speaking a prophecy 
help cause it?

---

## The Burned Idealist
Someone who believed in something completely, gave everything to it, 
and watched it fail — or worse, succeed in ways they never intended. 
They're not a villain yet. They're in the moment between idealism and 
cynicism, and which way they fall depends on what happens next.

*The House version:* Which floor produces the most Burned Idealists? 
What do they do when they have nowhere to fall back to?

---

## The Uncanny Survivor
The person who should have died and didn't. Who has outlasted everything 
around them — wars, factions, ages. They carry a weight that others 
don't understand: the weight of having been present for everything. 
Also: the guilt of having survived.

*The House version:* What does long life look like across floors? 
Does surviving change your floor affiliation?

---

## The Useful Monster
An entity that is genuinely dangerous and genuinely useful, in roughly 
equal measure. Not a villain to be defeated, not an ally to be trusted, 
but something the world keeps alive because the cost of killing it is 
higher than the cost of living with it.

*The House version:* What floor is this entity from? Does its floor 
of origin explain what it wants and why it's contained?
