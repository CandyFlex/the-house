# Framework — Floor Development

Use this when developing a floor from scratch or expanding a thin one.
Work through each section. You don't have to answer everything — blank 
is valid — but the questions you can't answer reveal what needs work.

---

## 1. Thematic Anchor

The color is the starting point, not the end point.

- What does [color] mean as an emotion or psychological state?
- What does [color] mean as a physical sensation?
- What mythology or symbolism surrounds this color across human cultures?
- Which of those resonances feel right for this floor? Which feel wrong?
- What is the *one thing* this floor is about, stated as a contradiction?
  (e.g., "Floor 5 is about the peace that destroys you" or 
  "Floor 1 is about the warmth that cannot be trusted.")

---

## 2. Scale Choice

Pick a scale and commit to it — it sets the texture of stories here.

- Continent (civilization-scale, political, geographical)
- Planet (biome diversity, orbital isolation, astronomical)
- Galaxy (vast distances, dead civilizations, light-lag communication)
- Multiverse (reality is plural, doors between versions, identity is unstable)
- Something else entirely

Note: Scale is non-Euclidean. You can pick "planet" and also have a 
district that contains a galaxy. But anchoring a dominant scale gives 
the floor its felt size.

---

## 3. Dominant Archetypes

What kinds of people, factions, and phenomena are *native* to this floor?

- What archetype does this floor produce in people who are born here?
- What kind of visitor does this floor attract from other floors?
- What kind of visitor does this floor repel or destroy?
- Name three factions that could only exist here (not on any other floor).
- What does power look like here? (Military? Knowledge? Lineage? Access?)

---

## 4. Relationship to Neighbors

Every floor touches two others (except 1 and 7, which touch one).

- What does this floor *give* to the floor below it?
- What does this floor *take* from the floor above it?
- What does this floor *refuse* to acknowledge about its neighbors?
- Is the border between this floor and its neighbors hard (clear threshold) 
  or soft (gradient, bleed, overlap)?

---

## 5. Tension and Fault Lines

A floor without internal tension has nothing to do in it.

- What is the central unresolved conflict of this floor?
- Who benefits from that conflict staying unresolved?
- What would have to change for the floor to fundamentally transform?
- What does this floor lie to itself about?

---

## 6. Entry and Exit

- How do people typically arrive on this floor?
- How do people typically leave?
- Is there anywhere on this floor where the border with another floor 
  is thin, unstable, or contested?
- Who controls access? Is access contested?

---

## 7. What's Missing

When you finish the above, note everything you couldn't answer. 
Those are the open questions. Put them in the floor seed file under 
`## Open Questions`. That's not failure — that's honest accounting.
