# Exercise — Five Ways a Faction Has Betrayed Itself

Every faction starts with a purpose. Every faction eventually betrays 
that purpose. This exercise finds the rot.

---

## The Exercise

Name a faction. Then write five ways it has betrayed its own founding 
purpose — one for each category below. Not all five may apply yet, 
especially for new factions. The ones that don't apply yet are 
predictions, not history. That's fine.

---

## The Five Categories

### 1. The Belief Betrayal
What did they used to believe that they no longer believe?
How are they still using the language of the old belief to describe 
the new one?

*Prompt:* "We still call it [X], but what we mean now is [Y]."

---

### 2. The Action Betrayal
What did they swear they would never do?
What made them do it the first time?
Is there anyone left in the faction who still feels bad about it?

*Prompt:* "The first time we did [Z], we told ourselves it was 
an exception. Now it's standard practice."

---

### 3. The Membership Betrayal
Who would the founders not recognize if they came back?
What kind of person does the faction attract now vs. at founding?
Has the faction been infiltrated, co-opted, or absorbed by something 
it was formed to oppose?

*Prompt:* "The people running it now joined because of [reason] — 
which has nothing to do with why it was founded."

---

### 4. The Ally Betrayal
Who is the faction's most important current ally?
Would the founders have accepted that alliance?
What has the faction compromised to maintain it?

*Prompt:* "We work with [them] now because we need [resource]. 
The founders would have called that a betrayal."

---

### 5. The Cost Betrayal
What does maintaining the faction now cost — in lives, resources, 
moral credibility, freedom of action?
Is that cost greater than the original problem the faction was 
formed to solve?
Who is paying the cost, and is it the same people who benefit?

*Prompt:* "We've spent more [cost] on keeping the faction alive 
than we have on [original mission] in the last [time period]."

---

## What To Do With This

These betrayals are not necessarily public. Some are known only to 
insiders. Some are known to no one — the faction has buried them so 
deeply that they've forgotten. 

After completing the five, ask: **Which of these betrayals, if exposed, 
would destroy the faction?** That's the story.
