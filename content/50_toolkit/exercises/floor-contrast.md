# Exercise — Floor Identity Through Contrast

A floor is most itself when it's next to something it isn't. Use 
this exercise to sharpen a floor's identity by defining it against 
its neighbors and against its opposite.

---

## Part 1: Against the Neighbor Below

Floor [N] sits above Floor [N-1].

- What does Floor [N-1] have that Floor [N] lacks?
- What does Floor [N] look down on about Floor [N-1]?
- What does Floor [N] secretly envy about Floor [N-1]?
- What would someone from Floor [N-1] find alien or uncomfortable 
  about Floor [N]?
- What flows upward from [N-1] to [N]? (Trade, people, ideas, monsters?)
- What does Floor [N] refuse to receive from Floor [N-1]?

---

## Part 2: Against the Neighbor Above

Floor [N] sits below Floor [N+1].

- What does Floor [N+1] have that Floor [N] aspires to?
- What does Floor [N] think is wrong or decadent about Floor [N+1]?
- What would someone from Floor [N+1] find beneath them about Floor [N]?
- What flows downward from [N+1] to [N]?
- What does Floor [N] refuse to send up to Floor [N+1]?

---

## Part 3: Against the Opposite

Assign an opposite floor based on thematic tension:
- Red (1) ↔ Violet (7) — entry vs. culmination
- Orange (2) ↔ Indigo (6) — warmth vs. cold knowledge
- Yellow (3) ↔ Blue (5) — clarity vs. depth
- Green (4) is the center — its opposite is itself, or The House as a whole.

- What does Floor [N] and its opposite share that neither will admit?
- What does Floor [N] have that its opposite can never have?
- What does Floor [N] want from its opposite?
- Has there ever been a war between them? A union? A trade?

---

## Part 4: The One-Sentence Answer

After working through the above, write one sentence that captures 
what Floor [N] is — not by describing it directly, but by describing 
what it is *not*:

"Floor [N] is everything Floor [N-1] wanted to become and everything 
Floor [N+1] has already forgotten."

Adjust as needed. The sentence should surprise you slightly. If it 
doesn't, the floor doesn't have enough identity yet.
