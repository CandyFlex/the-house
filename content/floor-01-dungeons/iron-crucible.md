# Iron Crucible

*Hex 5. Moderate (10 pts). Three room nodes → Boss. Active daily.*

---

## Brief

The Iron Crucible is a hellish forge-dungeon buried beneath a blackened volcanic fissure — a place where rivers of molten iron snake through stone channels, anvil-chambers ring with ceaseless hammer-falls, and corridors shimmer with heat-haze so thick it bends the walls. The aesthetic is brutal industrial fantasy: mountains of slag piled against pillars, quenching steam that hisses from cooling troughs, bellows the size of houses that breathe with a rhythm older than living memory, and cinder that drifts like snow through air you can taste. This is no ruin. The Crucible still runs.

It was Caesar's armory-foundry — the beating industrial heart that armed his legions for conquest across planes. Here, smiths worked in shifts that never ended, feeding ore into the central crucible and drawing finished blades from molds that remembered every specification Caesar ever demanded. The forge was designed to outlast the empire itself, driven by bound elemental heat and the unceasing labor of the damned. When the empire fell, the Crucible did not stop. It could not stop. The command woven into its foundation — "Forge until the empire falls" — became an irony of the cruelest kind: the empire is gone, but the forge keeps forging.

Now the Crucible is a haunted factory. The smiths who died at their anvils, worked to death or consumed by the heat they served, have fused into constructs of slag and iron — the Iron Husks — that still pour molten metal into waiting molds. Their collective ghost, the Forge-Wraith, stands at the central crucible and drives the hammer-song onward. The foundry produces weapons for an army that no longer exists, stockpiling blades and spearheads in chambers that have not been opened in centuries. It is hungry still — hungry for metal, hungry for flesh, hungry for purpose. Style ideology: Industry without conscience. The Crucible represents the machinery of oppression in its purest form — a forge that never asked what it was making, only that it make more.

## Public Façade

The approach to the Iron Crucible is visible from half a mile away: a great volcanic fissure splitting a blasted plain of black glass and rust-red cinders. Steam vents hiss continuously, sending white plumes spiraling into a sky stained orange by the glow rising from below. The sound of hammering echoes from the depths — a relentless, rhythmic metal-on-metal that never falters, never pauses, never rests. Heat-shimmer warps the air above the fissure into dancing curtains of distortion.

A stone ramp, wide enough for mine carts, spirals downward into the gloom along the fissure wall. Its surface is worn smooth by centuries of foot and wheel traffic, scored with deep ruts where ore carts once ran. Channels cut into the walls carry streams of molten metal — orange-white rivers that cast flickering light across the descent and radiate blistering heat. The ramp descends sixty feet before leveling out at a massive iron door embossed with a crossed hammer-and-anvil crest: the seal of Caesar's armory.

The door is unlocked — it was never meant to keep anyone out, only to contain the heat. Above it, carved into the obsidian arch, is the inscription: SERVILE MANUS / AETERNA FLAMMA — "Servant hands, eternal flame."

Daily cycle. The Crucible resets at dawn each day — slag is cleared by unseen hands, toppled vats right themselves, and the Iron Husks reform from scattered cinder. Weekly phase-lock: closed on Chainday. The forge cools for maintenance as ancient enchantments cycle down the elemental heat. Anyone inside when the lock occurs is sealed in for the full 24-hour cycle — no exit, no reprieve, and the ambient temperature drops only slightly.

## Admin Sub-Entrance

Three hundred yards east of the public entrance, a collapsed ore-delivery chute marks the admin entry point. The chute was designed to funnel raw iron ore from the surface down to the smelting level — a steep, angled shaft roughly four feet in diameter, now partially blocked by fallen obsidian and fused slag deposits. Navigating the chute requires a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check to squeeze through the narrowest point without dislodging the unstable debris. On a failure, the debris shifts: the character takes 2d6 bludgeoning damage from falling rock and must make a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw to avoid being pinned (restrained) until freed.

The chute opens into the Interstitial Layer — a narrow maintenance catwalk suspended above the main forge complex. This network of grated iron walkways, chain-ladders, and heat-shielded control booths was built for the Crucible's overseers to monitor production without descending to the floor. The catwalks run along the ceiling of every chamber, accessible through maintenance hatches that drop into alcoves, mold-storage rooms, and unused service tunnels. The Interstitial Layer is cooler than the forge floor — "cooler" meaning the air is merely 120 degrees instead of 400 — but the fumes are thicker here, collecting in the upper reaches where ventilation is poorest.

**Admin environmental challenges:**
- **Heat Exhaustion.** Every 15 minutes spent on the catwalk, a DC 13 Constitution saving throw is required. On a failure, the character gains 1 level of exhaustion. Fire resistance grants advantage on this save; fire immunity negates it entirely.
- **Unstable Catwalks.** Sections of the grating have corroded and weakened over centuries. Crossing these sections requires a DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check. On a failure, the grate gives way — the character falls 20 feet to the forge floor below, taking 2d6 bludgeoning damage plus 1d6 fire damage from contact with the superheated stone.
- **Toxic Fumes.** The upper layer concentrates sulfurous and metallic vapors. After 1 hour without a filtered breathing apparatus, characters must make a DC 12 Constitution save each hour or gain the poisoned condition for 1 hour.

**Corruption note:** The Crucible has begun producing items without input. Somewhere in the interstitial maintenance logs, overseers recorded instances of blades appearing in molds that had been empty moments before — the forge's memory of Caesar's armory specifications is so deeply embedded that it now materializes weapons from ambient heat and iron vapor. These "ghost-forged" items are indistinguishable from normally smithed ones except for their temperature: they never fully cool. The admin can adjust this behavior from the maintenance booth behind the boss chamber, toggling the ghost-production rate between 0% (off) and the current unmonitored setting of approximately 15% of total output.

## Room Nodes

### Node 1 — The Smelting Gallery

**Public experience:** The doors open into a long, vaulted hall — a hundred feet of stone lit only by the orange-white glow of six massive smelting vats, three on each side, each tended by a silent guardian. The Iron Husks are humanoid constructs made of riveted plates, scorched leather, and exposed iron framework — suits of animated armor whose every joint leaks a thin trickle of molten light. They pour endless streams of liquid metal from crucible-ladles into sand-molds that shape swords, spearheads, arrowheads, and shield-bosses — the standard kit of Caesar's legionaries. The molds hiss and steam as the metal sets, and the finished products are stacked in iron racks along the walls, thousands of identical blades waiting for hands that will never claim them. The air is thick with the smell of hot iron, cinder, and ozone. The Iron Husks will ignore anyone who presents a forge-crest — a small iron badge worn on the chest, visible on each Husk. Anyone without a crest is an intruder, and the Husks will attack in unison, their movements grinding but terrifyingly precise.

**Room challenge:** Combat encounter with 3–4 Iron Husks. Use **animated armor** statistics with the following additions: **Heated Body** — any creature that starts its turn within 5 feet of an Iron Husk takes 1d6 fire damage. **Molten Core** — when reduced to 0 HP, the Husk's inner furnace ruptures in a 10-foot radius burst of molten slag (2d4 fire damage, DC 12 Dexterity save for half). The smelting vats are environmental hazards — they can be toppled with a DC 14 Strength (Athletics) check, creating a 15-foot-square pool of molten metal on the floor. The pool is difficult terrain that deals 2d6 fire damage for every 5 feet moved through it and remains for 3 rounds before cooling to a solid, impassable slag deposit.

**Admin options:**
- From the interstitial catwalk above the Smelting Gallery, the Iron Husks' forge-crests are visible from above — small octagonal iron badges riveted to each Husk's chest plate. A control panel mounted on the catwalk railing (DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana)) allows the admin to disable the crest-recognition protocol, causing the Husks to stand idle and ignore all creatures regardless of badge status.
- A maintenance hatch on the eastern catwalk drops directly into the mold-storage alcove — a small side chamber stacked with sand molds and finished blades, completely out of the Husks' patrol zones. No combat required to bypass this room.

### Node 2 — The Anvil Choir

**Public experience:** The passage from the Smelting Gallery opens into a circular chamber — the Anvil Choir. Twenty anvils ring the room on raised stone daises, each one a massive block of scarred black iron. No Husks tend them. No hands grip the hammers. Yet each anvil is being struck, over and over, by a disembodied hammer that floats in the air above it — phantoms of iron and will, invisible save for the tools they wield. The hammers rise and fall in a shared rhythm, a metallic cadence that fills the chamber with a sound you feel in your teeth. At first it seems like noise; then, as your mind strains to find pattern in chaos, patterns emerge — a marching beat, a war-drum call, the death-knell of ten thousand battles hammered into metal. The rhythm is the smith's song, and it is maddening.

**Room challenge:** 4–5 Hammer Poltergeists. Use **poltergeist** statistics with the following modifications: the poltergeists are invisible (as the spell), but the hammers they wield are fully visible and serve as their object to animate. Each poltergeist is bound to one specific anvil — destroying that anvil banishes the poltergeist. At initiative count 20 each round (losing ties), the hammers strike in unison. All creatures in the chamber must make a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, the creature is affected as by the *confusion* spell for 1 round (roll on the confusion table as normal). A creature that succeeds on this save is immune to the Hammer Choir's maddening rhythm for the next hour. Anvils can be destroyed — each has AC 15, 20 HP, and immunity to poison and psychic damage; destroying an anvil (not merely damaging it) removes the poltergeist bound to it.

**Admin options:**
- From the catwalk above the Anvil Choir, the hammer sequence's pattern becomes visible with patient observation. Spending 3 rounds studying the rhythm (DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)) reveals the underlying sequence — a 20-beat loop tied to the forge's operational cadence. Once known, the admin automatically succeeds on the Wisdom save against the maddening rhythm for the remainder of their time in the Crucible.
- The catwalk is equipped with five release levers controlling counterweight blocks suspended above the anvils. Each lever can be thrown as an action to drop a counterweight onto a specific anvil, destroying it remotely and banishing its poltergeist. After dropping, the counterweights cannot be reset — only 2 of the 5 still have functional release mechanisms. Choose wisely which anvils to destroy.

### Node 3 — The Heat Shimmer Corridor

**Public experience:** Beyond the Anvil Choir lies a narrow passage — only 5 feet wide, with walls that glow cherry-red from the heat radiating through them. The air ripples and bends, creating a maze of shimmering illusions that shifts and reforms every few seconds. What looks like solid stone is vapor; what appears to be open passage is searing iron. The walls themselves seem to breathe, pulsing with waves of heat that distort distance and direction. The correct path — the true path — shimmers differently than the false ones, but the difference is subtle, a gradation of heat and light that requires focus to perceive. Touch a wrong wall, and the heat discharges through your body in a white-hot flash — 2d6 fire damage as the corridor rejects you, teleporting you back to the entrance of the passage. Creatures that rely primarily on sight have disadvantage on all checks made to navigate the corridor.

**Room challenge:** A skill challenge requiring 3 successes before 3 failures. The party must collectively navigate the shifting path. Options include:
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Survival):** Track the true path by sensing minute temperature differences where the correct route is fractionally cooler than the illusions.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation):** Discern the shimmer pattern — the true path's heat-waves oscillate at a slightly different frequency than the false walls.
- **Creative alternatives:** *Detect magic* reveals the teleportation wards on the false walls, highlighting the correct route. A character with fire resistance can simply close their eyes and feel along with a gloved hand (DC 12 with fire resistance, but takes half damage on failure instead of full). Any water or coolant-based spell (*create water*, *ray of frost*) temporarily clears the shimmer in a 10-foot radius for one round.

Each failure deals 1d6 fire damage to the character attempting the check and resets their position to the corridor entrance. Characters who have already contributed a success cannot attempt further checks unless all party members have attempted and the group still needs successes.

**Admin options:**
- The heat-shimmer is powered by a central furnace core, accessible from the interstitial layer directly above the corridor. A panel on the catwalk allows venting the accumulated heat through the ceiling (DC 12 Intelligence (Arcana) as an action), pulling the superheated air upward and dissipating the shimmer illusions entirely for 1 minute — long enough for an entire party to walk through unhindered.
- A vertical maintenance ladder on the catwalk bypasses the Heat Shimmer Corridor entirely, dropping through a service shaft that opens directly into the antechamber before the Boss Chamber. The ladder is intact but slick with condensed metallic vapor — DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) to descend without slipping and falling the final 10 feet.

## Boss Chamber — The Crucible Heart

**Public experience:** The service shaft or corridor opens onto a ledge overlooking the final chamber — the Crucible Heart. The space is immense: a natural volcanic vault, its walls glowing with veins of molten rock, its ceiling lost in smoke and steam a hundred feet overhead. Below, a lake of molten iron stretches from wall to wall, its surface a restless orange-white mirror broken by belching gas bubbles and erupting plumes of liquid metal. At the center of the lake, suspended by four massive iron chains anchored to the walls, hangs the Crucible itself — a basin of black iron fifty feet across, filled with metal so bright it hurts to look at directly. Steam rises from it in a continuous hissing column.

Standing at the Crucible's rim is the Forge-Wraith. It is fifteen feet tall — a spectral giant of smoke, cinder, and white-hot rage, its form a permanent silhouette against the glow of molten iron. It wears a massive leather apron, scorched black and crackling with embers, and holds a hammer the size of a barn door, its head glowing from within like a dying star. The Wraith's face is not one face but many — a shifting composite of every smith who died in this place, their features flickering in and out of the smoke like faces in a bonfire: a hollow-eyed foreman, a teenage apprentice whose hands never hardened to the heat, an old master smith who refused to leave his anvil even as his lungs filled with iron vapor. The Forge-Wraith is their collective ghost, fused by the Crucible's unyielding imperative into a single being whose only purpose is to keep forging.

**Forge-Wraith (CR 11).** Use **fire giant** statistics as a base, with the following modifications:
- **Incorporeal Movement.** The Forge-Wraith can move through other creatures and objects as if they were difficult terrain. It takes 1d10 force damage if it ends its turn inside an object.
- **Damage Immunities:** Fire, poison.
- **Damage Vulnerabilities:** Cold.
- **Condition Immunities:** Charmed, frightened, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned.
- **Smiting Song.** Audible throughout the chamber — the Forge-Wraith's hammering rhythm imposes disadvantage on Constitution saving throws against heat exhaustion for all creatures that can hear it.

**Legendary Actions (3 per round):**
- **Hammer Sweep (2 actions).** The Forge-Wraith swings its hammer in a 15-foot cone. Each creature in the area must make a DC 17 Dexterity saving throw, taking 3d8 bludgeoning + 3d8 fire damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success.
- **Cinder Cloud (1 action).** The Forge-Wraith exhales a cloud of superheated ash and cinder in a 15-foot radius centered on itself. The area becomes heavily obscured until the start of its next turn. Any creature that enters the cloud or starts its turn there takes 2d6 fire damage.
- **Anvil Drop (1 action).** Ranged attack (60 ft). The Forge-Wraith hurls a spectral anvil at one creature. The target must make a DC 17 Dexterity saving throw or be restrained under the phantom anvil until the end of the Wraith's next turn. The target can use its action to make a DC 17 Strength check to break free.

**Lair Actions (Initiative 20, losing ties):**
- **Bellows' Exhalation.** The great forge bellows mounted in the walls exhale with a deafening roar. All creatures in the chamber must make a DC 14 Strength saving throw or be pushed 15 feet directly away from the nearest wall, regardless of what lies in that direction — often toward the molten lake or the edge of the ledge.
- **Molten Geyser.** A plume of molten iron erupts from the lake. Choose a 10-foot-radius area within 60 feet of the Wraith. Each creature in the area must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw, taking 2d8 fire damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. The affected area becomes difficult terrain until the start of the Wraith's next turn as the cooling metal hardens into jagged slag.

**Environmental hazard:** The molten iron lake deals 4d6 fire damage per round to any creature immersed in it. A creature that starts its turn in the lake also takes 1d6 fire damage at the start of its next turn as clinging metal continues to burn. The ledge ringing the chamber is 15 feet above the lake surface — a creature pushed off takes no falling damage from the drop (molten metal cushions the fall) but must deal with immersion immediately.

**Admin Override:**
- The Forge-Wraith was bound to the Crucible by Caesar's command, spoken at the forge's consecration: "Forge until the empire falls." This phrase, inscribed on a brass plaque in the heat-shielded maintenance booth, became the binding command that holds the Wraith in place. Since Caesar's empire has, by all meaningful measures, fallen — speaking the phrase in Latin aloud in the Boss Chamber (**"Cude donec imperium cadat"**) releases the Wraith from its compulsion. The Forge-Wraith will stop mid-swing, turn its composite face toward the speaker, and — for the first time in centuries — lay down its hammer. It will speak through its many mouths in unison, offering a single word of thanks before its form disperses into harmless cinder and smoke. This bypasses combat entirely.
- The Crucible's master control station is located in a heat-shielded maintenance booth accessible through a reinforced door behind the boss chamber (on the ledge, opposite the entry point). The booth contains three control arrays:
  - **Husk Spawn Rate.** A dial that adjusts Iron Husk population density in the Smelting Gallery from 1 to 5 active Husks per chamber refresh.
  - **Heat Level.** A lever controlling the ambient forge temperature, which adjusts all fire damage dice in the dungeon up or down by one die size (d4 to d6, d6 to d8, etc.). Minimum: d4. Maximum: d12.
  - **Loot Pool Weighting.** A set of brass toggles that shift which categories of items are more likely to appear in the Crucible's ghost-forged production cycles — weapons, armor, wondrous items, or rings.
